# The Criminal Procedure Rules 2025, section 17.8

> Extent: England, Wales.

## 17.8. Taking a deposition in a magistrates’ court[^key-a57a50ff8144b02a3aa18c3137ddfa01]

*Extent: E+W*

(1) This rule applies where a witness attends or is brought before a magistrates’ court on a summons or warrant issued under paragraph 4 of Schedule 3 to the Crime and Disorder Act 1998[^f00435]—

- (a) to have evidence taken as a deposition; or
- (b) to produce a document or other exhibit.

(2) The general rule is that the hearing must be in public but the court may exercise any power it has to—

- (a) impose a restriction on reporting what takes place, or public access;
- (b) withhold information from the public during the hearing; or
- (c) order a hearing in private.

(3) The court must, in this sequence—

- (a) explain, in terms the witness can understand (with help, if necessary), that—
  - (i) the court is required by law to take evidence for use by the prosecutor in the Crown Court,
  - (ii) any document or other exhibit that the witness was required to produce by the summons or warrant to attend must be produced to the court unless there is a just excuse for not doing so,
  - (iii) the prosecutor will ask questions that the witness must answer unless there is a just excuse for not doing so,
  - (iv) the witness’ evidence will be recorded in writing in a document called a ‘deposition’ which will be read to the witness and which the witness will be asked to sign, and
  - (v) if the witness refuses to produce a required document or other exhibit, or refuses to answer a question, without just excuse, then the court may commit the witness to custody and may impose a fine;
- (b) take possession of any document or other exhibit that the witness was required to produce by the summons or warrant to attend;
- (c) require the prosecutor to question the witness;
- (d) allow any legal representative of the witness who is present to question the witness;
- (e) as a general rule, allow a legal representative of the defendant, if present, to question the witness but only to the extent reasonably required to clarify the witness’ answer to a question asked by the prosecutor;
- (f) cause the witness’ evidence to be recorded in writing in a deposition;
- (g) in the event of refusal by the witness to produce a required document or other exhibit, or to answer a question—
  - (i) ask the witness to explain why,
  - (ii) consider the explanation,
  - (iii) if satisfied that there is a just excuse for refusal, cause a record of that conclusion to be included in the deposition,
  - (iv) if not so satisfied and the refusal is maintained, deal with the refusal at the end of the hearing; and
- (h) certify the accuracy of the deposition by—
  - (i) inviting the witness to authenticate it, by any written or electronic means, and
  - (ii) whether the witness agrees to do so or not, authenticating the deposition by any such means itself.

(4) A justices’ legal adviser must assist the court by preparing the deposition, unless the court—

- (a) includes a District Judge (Magistrates’ Courts); and
- (b) otherwise directs.

(5) The magistrates’ court officer must, as soon as practicable, make available to the Crown Court and serve on the prosecutor—

- (a) a copy of the deposition;
- (b) a copy of any document produced by the witness; and
- (c) a description of any object produced by the witness.

*\[Note. For the functions of a justices’ legal adviser, see sections 28 and 29 of the Courts Act 2003*[^f00436]*. See also rule 2.12 (Duties of justices’ legal adviser).\]*
