# The Criminal Procedure Rules 2025, section 17.2

> **Warning: changes not yet applied to this text (20).** Text as at 2026-10-05. See https://www.legislation.gov.uk/changes/affected/uksi/2025/909
>
> Extent: England, Wales.

## 17.2. Issue etc. of summons, warrant or order[^key-1a4e0bb7d198a794e831888d659a4070]

*Extent: E+W*

(1) The court may issue or withdraw a witness summons, warrant or order—

- (a) at a hearing, which must be in private unless the court otherwise directs; or
- (b) without a hearing.

(2) A summons or warrant issued under section 97 of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980 or under section 2 or 4 of the Criminal Procedure (Attendance of Witnesses) Act 1965 must specify—

- (a) the time and place at which the witness must attend the court to give evidence; and
- (b) any document or thing that the witness then must produce.

(3) A warrant issued under section 97 of the 1980 Act may specify a period, not exceeding one month, for which the witness may be committed to custody for refusal without just excuse—

- (a) to give evidence; or
- (b) to produce any document or thing required.

(4) A summons or warrant issued under paragraph 4 of Schedule 3 to the Crime and Disorder Act 1998—

- (a) must specify—
  - (i) the time and place at which the witness must attend the court to have evidence taken as a deposition, and
  - (ii) any document or other exhibit that the witness then must produce; and
- (b) may specify a period, not exceeding one month, for which the witness may be committed to custody for refusal without just excuse—
  - (i) to have evidence taken as a deposition, or
  - (ii) to produce any such document or other exhibit.

*\[Note. If rule 17.5 applies, a person served with an application for a witness summons will have an opportunity to make representations about whether there should be a hearing of that application before the witness summons is issued.*

*Under section 97(4) of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980, a warrant issued under section 97(2) or (3) may specify a maximum period of potential committal to custody, up to one month, for refusal without just excuse to be sworn or give evidence, or to produce any document or thing required.*

*Under paragraph 4(6) of Schedule 3 to the Crime and Disorder Act 1998, the time specified for attendance in a summons or warrant issued under that paragraph must be such as to allow a deposition to be taken before time expires for the service of prosecution evidence in the Crown Court. Under paragraph 4(7), the summons or warrant may specify a maximum period of potential committal to custody, up to one month, for refusal without just excuse to have evidence taken as a deposition, or to produce a document or other exhibit required.\]*
