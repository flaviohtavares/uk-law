# The Criminal Procedure Rules 2025, section 46.1

> Extent: England, Wales.

## 46.1. Functions of representatives and supporters[^c-368bd9638dc727cdf839000377526c15]

*Extent: E+W*

(1) Under these Rules, anything that a party may or must do may be done—

- (a) by a legal representative on that party’s behalf;
- (b) by a person with the corporation’s written authority, where that corporation is a defendant; or
- (c) with the help of a parent, guardian or other suitable supporting adult where that party is a defendant—
  - (i) who is under 18, or
  - (ii) whose understanding of what the case involves is limited

unless other legislation (including a rule) otherwise requires.

(2) A member, officer or employee of a prosecutor may, on the prosecutor’s behalf—

- (a) serve on the magistrates’ court officer, or present to a magistrates’ court, an application for a summons or warrant under section 1 of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980[^f01095]; or
- (b) issue a written charge and requisition, or single justice procedure notice, under section 29 of the Criminal Justice Act 2003[^f01096].

*\[Note. See also section 122 of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980*[^f01097]*. A party’s legal representative must be entitled to act as such under section 13 of the Legal Services Act 2007*[^f01098]*.*

*Section 33(6) of the Criminal Justice Act 1925*[^f01099]*, section 46 of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980*[^f01100] *and Schedule 3 to that Act*[^f01101] *provide for the representation of a corporation.*

*Sections 3 and 6 of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985*[^f01102] *make provision about the institution of prosecutions.*

*Section 223 of the Local Government Act 1972*[^f01103] *allows a member or officer of a local authority on that authority’s behalf to prosecute or defend a case before a magistrates’ court, and to appear in and to conduct any proceedings before a magistrates’ court.*

*Part 7 contains rules about starting a prosecution.\]*
