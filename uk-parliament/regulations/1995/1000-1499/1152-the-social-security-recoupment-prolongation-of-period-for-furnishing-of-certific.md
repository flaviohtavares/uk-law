# The Social Security (Recoupment) (Prolongation of Period for Furnishing of Certificate of Total Benefit) Order 1995

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

1995 No. 1152

SOCIAL SECURITY

The Social Security (Recoupment) (Prolongation of Period for Furnishing of Certificate of Total Benefit) Order 1995

Made

26th April 1995

Laid before Parliament

26th April 1995

Coming into force

27th April 1995

The Secretary of State for Social Security, in exercise of the power conferred upon him by section 96(4) of the Social Security Administration Act 1992[^f00001], and of all other powers enabling him in that behalf, being of the opinion that circumstances have arisen which adversely affect normal methods of communication, hereby makes the following Order:

## 1. Citation and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Social Security (Recoupment) (Prolongation of Period for Furnishing of Certificate of Total Benefit) Order 1995 and shall come into force on 27th April 1995.

## 2. Prolongation of Period for Furnishing of Certificate of Total Benefit for the Purposes of Determining whether Liability has Ceased to be Enforceable

No liability of a compensator under section 82 of the Social Security Administration Act 1992 shall become unenforceable by virtue of section 96(1)(c) of that Act during a period of three months commencing on the date this Order comes into force.

Signed by authority of the Secretary of State for Social Security.

Roger Evans

Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State,Department of Social Security

26th April 1995

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order provides that, for the time limit designated in section 96(1)(c) of the Social Security Administration Act 1992 for the furnishing of a certificate of total benefit, namely that referred to in section 95(1) of that Act, there shall be substituted a period of three months commencing on the date this Order comes into force.

This Order does not impose any costs on business.
