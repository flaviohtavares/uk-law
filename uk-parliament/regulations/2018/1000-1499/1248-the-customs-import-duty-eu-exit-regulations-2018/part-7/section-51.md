# The Customs (Import Duty) (EU Exit) Regulations 2018, section 51

> **Warning: changes not yet applied to this text (4).** Text as at 2026-06-30. See https://www.legislation.gov.uk/changes/affected/uksi/2018/1248
>
> Extent: England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland.

## 51. Defective and non-compliant chargeable goods[^c-8e4e7cfdaf0a16217b8d229f8cdf6b50][^c-302606d159bff3b39c5b1dbf0584ce62][^c-302606d159bff3b39c5b1dbf0584ce62]

*Extent: E+W+S+N.I.*

(1) Subject to paragraph (3), paragraph (4) applies where—

- (a) chargeable goods are released [^c23522021] to a Customs procedure;
- (b) a contract was entered into for the sale and purchase of the goods before that release; and
- (c) the buyer notified the seller that the goods were rejected as not being in compliance with the contract as soon as practicable after the grounds for rejection became known to the buyer.

(2) In paragraph (1)(c)—

- (a) grounds for rejection include where the goods are defective or damaged; and
- (b) the rejection may occur after the goods cease to be chargeable goods.

(3) Paragraph (4) does not apply where—

- (a) the goods have been used or processed, other than that which was necessary to determine if the goods were in compliance with the contract; or
- (b) before the goods were released to a Customs procedure, the goods were declared for a special Customs procedure [^c23522031] in order to be tested to determine if the goods were in compliance with the contract, unless such testing would not normally have revealed such compliance.

(4) The amount of import duty in respect of the rejected goods is the amount which may be remitted or repaid.
