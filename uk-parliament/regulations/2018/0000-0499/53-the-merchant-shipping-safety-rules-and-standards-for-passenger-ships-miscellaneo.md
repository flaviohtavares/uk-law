# The Merchant Shipping (Safety Rules and Standards for Passenger Ships) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2018

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

This Statutory Instrument has been made in part in consequence of defects in [S.I. 2015/508](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/508) and [S.I. 2012/2636](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/2636) and is being issued free of charge to all known recipients of those Statutory Instruments.

2018 No. 53

Merchant Shipping

The Merchant Shipping (Safety Rules and Standards for Passenger Ships) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2018

Made

16th January 2018

Laid before Parliament

24th January 2018

Coming into force

15th February 2018

The Secretary of State makes the following Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 85(1), (3), (5) to (7), and 86(1) of the Merchant Shipping Act 1995[^f00001] and section 2(2) of the European Communities Act 1972[^f00002].

The Secretary of State has consulted the persons referred to in section 86(4) of the Merchant Shipping Act 1995.

The Secretary of State is a Minister designated[^f00003] for the purposes of section 2(2) of the European Communities Act 1972 in relation to measures relating to the safety of ships, and the health and safety of persons on them.

## 1. Citation and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Merchant Shipping (Safety Rules and Standards for Passenger Ships) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2018 and come into force on 15th February 2018.

## 2. Amendment of the Merchant Shipping (Survey and Certification) Regulations 2015

In regulation 3 (interpretation) of the Merchant Shipping (Survey and Certification) Regulations 2015[^f00004]—

- (a) for the definition of “the Directive” substitute—

  > - “the Directive” means [Directive 2009/45/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2009/0045) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 6th May 2009 on safety rules and standards for passenger ships[^f00005];

  , and
- (b) for the definition of “pleasure vessel” substitute—

  > - “pleasure vessel” means—
  >   - (a) any vessel which at the time it is being used is—
  >     - (i) in the case of a vessel wholly owned by—
  >       - (aa) an individual or individuals, used only for the sport or pleasure of the owner or the immediate family or friends of the owner; or
  >       - (bb) a body corporate, used only for sport or pleasure and on which the persons on board are employees or officers of the body corporate, or their immediate family or friends; and
  >     - (ii) on a voyage or excursion which is one for which the owner does not receive money for or in connection with operating the vessel or carrying any person, other than as a contribution to the direct expenses of the operation of the vessel incurred during the voyage or excursion; or
  >   - (b) any vessel wholly owned by or on behalf of a members’ club formed for the purpose of sport or pleasure which, at the time it is being used, is used only for the sport or pleasure of members of that club or their immediate family, and for the use of which any charges levied are paid into club funds and applied for the general use of the club,
  >
  >   where, in the case of any vessel referred to in paragraph (a) or (b), no other payments are made by or on behalf of users of the vessel, other than by the owner; and in this definition “immediate family” means, in relation to an individual, the spouse or civil partner of the individual, and a relative of the individual or the individual’s spouse or civil partner; and “relative” means brother, sister, ancestor or lineal descendant;.

  .

## 3. Amendment of the Merchant Shipping (Technical Requirements for Inland Waterway Vessels) Regulations 2010

In regulation 3(3)(b) of the Merchant Shipping (Technical Requirements for Inland Waterway Vessels) Regulations 2010[^f00006], for “[Directive 2009/45/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2009/0045)” until the end substitute “[Directive 2009/45/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2009/0045) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 6th May 2009 on safety rules and standards for passenger ships, or”.

## 4. Amendment of the Merchant Shipping (Passenger Ships on Domestic Voyages) Regulations 2000

(1) The Merchant Shipping (Passenger Ships on Domestic Voyages) Regulations 2000[^f00007] are amended as follows.

(2) In regulation 2 (interpretation)—

- (a) for the definition of “the Directive” substitute—

  > - “the Directive” means [Directive 2009/45/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2009/0045) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 6th May 2009 on safety rules and standards for passenger ships;

  ;
- (b) omit the definition of “EEA Agreement”;
- (c) for the definition of “EEA State” substitute—

  > - “EEA State” has the meaning given by Schedule 1 to the Interpretation Act 1978[^f00008]

  ,
- (d) for paragraph (2A) substitute—

  > (2A) References in the Directive to Conventions or Codes in their up to date versions which—
  >
  > - (a) relate to all or any of the purposes set out in section 85(1) of the Merchant Shipping Act 1995;
  > - (b) are considered by the Secretary of State to be relevant from time to time, and
  > - (c) are specified in a Merchant Shipping Note
  >
  > have effect in so far as they are so specified.

  .

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations implement Commission [Directive 2016/844/EU](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2016/0844) (OJ L 141, 28.5.2016, p.51) (“the Commission Directive”), which amends [Directive 2009/45/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2009/0045) (OJ L 163, 25.6.2009, p.1) (“the 2009 Directive”) on safety rules and standards for passenger ships. The Commission Directive makes several technical amendments to Annex I of the 2009 Directive.

Regulations 2, 3 and 4 each replace the definition of the 2009 Directive in order to incorporate the technical amendments to the 2009 Directive made by the Commission Directive. Regulation 2 makes this amendment in the Merchant Shipping (Survey and Certification) Regulations 2015 ([S.I. 2015/508](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/508)) (“the 2015 Regulations”). Regulation 3 does the same in the Merchant Shipping (Technical Requirements for Inland Waterway Vessels) Regulations 2010 ([S.I. 2010/1075](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/1075)) and Regulation 4 does the same in the Merchant Shipping (Passenger Ships on Domestic Voyages) Regulations 2000 ([S.I. 2000/2687](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2000/2687)) (“the 2000 Regulations”). This change is made in conjunction with a new Marine Guidance Note, MGN 572, “Safety Rules and Standards for Seagoing Domestic Passenger Ships: Directive (EU) 2016/844 Amendments”, which is available online at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mgn-572-m-safety-rules-and-standards-for-seagoing-domestic-passenger-ships and is available for inspection at the Maritime and Coastguard Agency, 105 Commercial Rd, Southampton SO15 1EG.

Regulation 2 inserts a slightly revised definition of “pleasure vessel” in the 2015 Regulations. Regulation 4 replaces previous definitions of “EEA State” and “EEA Agreement” with the definition of “EEA State” used in the Interpretation Act 1978, and replaces Regulation 2(2A) in the 2000 Regulations.

The Explanatory Memorandum published alongside this instrument is available on the website http://www.legislation.gov.uk. Copies are available for inspection at the Maritime and Coastguard Agency, at the address above.
