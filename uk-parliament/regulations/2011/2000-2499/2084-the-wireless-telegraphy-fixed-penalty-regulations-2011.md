# The Wireless Telegraphy (Fixed Penalty) Regulations 2011

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2011 No. 2084

Electronic Communications

The Wireless Telegraphy (Fixed Penalty) Regulations 2011

Made

23rd August 2011

Laid before Parliament

25th August 2011

Coming into force

1st October 2011

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred by section 96 of, and paragraph 2 of Schedule 4 to, the Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006[^f00001], makes the following Regulations:

## 1. Citation and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Wireless Telegraphy (Fixed Penalty) Regulations 2011 and shall come into force on 1st October 2011.

## 2. Fixed penalty offence

These Regulations apply to any offence under the Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006 (other than Part 4) which is a summary offence and which is committed after these Regulations come into force.

## 3. Amount of fixed penalty

The fixed penalty for an offence to which regulation 2 applies is £100.

Jeremy Hunt

Secretary of State for

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations do not create any new criminal offence but prescribe the amount of the fixed penalty which is to apply to any summary offence under the Wireless and Telegraphy Act 2006 (“the Act”) (other than Part 4), which is committed after these Regulations come into force.

Paragraph 2(1) of Schedule 4 to the Act enables the Secretary of State to prescribe the fixed penalty for a relevant offence. ‘Relevant offence’ is defined in paragraph 1 of Schedule 4 as an offence under the Act (other than under Part 4) which is a summary offence.

Regulation 3 provides that the fixed penalty is to be £100. Paragraph 2(2) of Schedule 4 to the Act provides that the amount of the fixed penalty cannot be more than 25 per cent of the maximum fine on summary conviction for the offence in question. Maximum fines for relevant offences range from Level 3 (£1,000) to Level 5 (£5,000).

A full regulatory impact assessment for these fixed penalty notices was published in December 2005 (http://www.bis.gov.uk/files/file15286.pdf). The Better Regulation Executive have advised that under current policy a formal impact assessment is not required. The impact assessment published in December 2005 is available from the Department for Culture, Media and Sport, Information Economy Division, 2-4 Cockspur Street, London SW1Y 5DH and is also available electronically at www.culture.gov.uk.
