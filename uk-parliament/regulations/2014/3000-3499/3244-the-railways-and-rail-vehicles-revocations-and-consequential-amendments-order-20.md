# The Railways and Rail Vehicles (Revocations and Consequential Amendments) Order 2014

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

Disabled Persons

Made

8th December 2014

Laid before Parliament

12th December 2014

Coming into force

5th January 2015

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred by the provisions set out in Schedule 1, makes the following Order.

The Secretary of State has consulted the Office of Rail Regulation so far as is required by section 7(1) of the Railways Act 1993[^f00001] for the purposes of this Order.

The Secretary of State has consulted the Disabled Persons Transport Advisory Committee[^f00002] and such other persons as the Secretary of State considers appropriate so far as is required by section 183(4) of the Equality Act 2010[^f00003] for the purposes of this Order.

In accordance with section 184(1), (2) and (3) of the Equality Act 2010, and regulation 2(1) and (3)(c) of the Rail Vehicle Accessibility Exemption Orders (Parliamentary Procedures) Regulations 2008[^f00004], and having had regard to representations by the Disabled Persons Transport Advisory Committee after consultation under section 184(2) of that Act, the Secretary of State has decided, so far as it is relevant to this Order, that this Order is to be subject to annulment in pursuance of a resolution of either House of Parliament.

## 1. Citation and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Railways and Rail Vehicles (Revocations and Consequential Amendments) Order 2014 and comes into force on 5th January 2015.

## 2. Revocations

The Orders listed in Schedule 2 are revoked.

## 3. Amendment of Order

(1) The Rail Vehicle Accessibility (Croydon Tramlink Class CR4000 Vehicles) Exemption Order 2001[^f00005] is amended as follows.

(2) In article 4 insert “and” at the end of paragraph (2)(b).

(3) Omit articles 4(2)(c), 7, 7A and 9.

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order revokes *(article 2)* six instruments relating to rail transport and makes consequential amendments *(article 3)* arising from one of the revocations.

The Railways Act 1993 (Consequential Modifications) (No. 3) Order 1994 ([S.I. 1994/2229](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1994/2229)) modified the Regulation of Railways Act [1871 (c. 78)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1871/78) (“the 1871 Act”) in consequence of provisions in the 1993 Act and instruments made under or by virtue of it. The 1871 Act has been repealed and this instrument is spent.

The Railways (Heathrow Express Temporary Network) (Exemptions) Order 1997 ([S.I. 1997/1531](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/1531)) exempted a temporary network, used during construction of a railway line to Heathrow Airport, from the franchising and closure provisions in the Railways Act [1993 (c. 43)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/43) (“the 1993 Act”). The Railways (Closure Provisions) (Exemptions) (St. Pancras) Order 2001 ([S.I. 2001/1768](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/1768)) provided that specified provisions in the 1993 Act did not apply to St. Pancras station and certain railway lines within the vicinity of that station. Both Orders were made in connection with work being carried out on the railway network. This work is now complete and the Orders are no longer required.

The Rail Vehicle Accessibility (Serco Metrolink T68A Vehicles) Exemption Order 1999 ([S.I. 1999/2932](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1999/2932)) (“the Metrolink Order”) and the Rail Vehicle Accessibility (Croydon Tramlink Class CR4000 Vehicles) Exemption (Amendment) Order 2005 ([S.I. 2005/395](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/395)) (“the Croydon Order”) exempted specified rail vehicles from particular requirements of the Rail Vehicle Accessibility Regulations 1998 ([S.I. 1998/2456](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/2456)) (“the 1998 Regulations”). The exemptions subsequently took effect as exemptions from requirements of the Rail Vehicle Accessibility (Non-Interoperable Rail System) Regulations 2010 ([S.I. 2010/432](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/432)) (see regulation 7(2)). The exemptions granted by the Croydon Order are spent because the vehicles are now compliant with the relevant requirements. The Metrolink Order is spent because the vehicles are compliant with the relevant requirements, and have also now been withdrawn from service.

The Strategic Rail Authority (Licence Exemption) Order 2001 ([S.I. 2001/218](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/218)) exempted the Strategic Rail Authority and its wholly owned subsidiaries from the requirement to hold a railway operators’ licence under section 6 of the 1993 Act. The Strategic Rail Authority was abolished in 2006 (see Part 1 of the Railways Act [2005 (c. 14)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/14) and article 2 of [S.I. 2006/2925](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2006/2925)) and this Order is spent.

*Article 3* of this Order makes consequential amendments to the Rail Vehicle Accessibility (Croydon Tramlink Class CR4000 Vehicles) Exemption Order 2001 ([S.I. 2001/3952](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/3952)) arising from the revocation of the Croydon Order.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument because no impact on business or the voluntary sector is foreseen. The Explanatory Memorandum is published alongside the instrument on www.legislation.gov.uk.
