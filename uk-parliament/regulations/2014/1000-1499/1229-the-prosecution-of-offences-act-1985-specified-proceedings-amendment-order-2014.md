# The Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 (Specified Proceedings) (Amendment) Order 2014

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2014 No. 1229

Criminal Law, England And Wales

The Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 (Specified Proceedings) (Amendment) Order 2014

Made

12 May 2014

Laid before Parliament

13 May 2014

Coming into force

9th June 2014

The Attorney General, in exercise of the powers conferred by section 3(3) of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985[^f00001], makes the following Order.

## 1. Citation and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 (Specified Proceedings) (Amendment) Order 2014 and comes into force on 9th June 2014.

## 2. Amendment of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 (Specified Proceedings) Order 1999

(1) The Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 (Specified Proceedings) Order 1999[^f00002] is amended as follows.

(2) In Part 1 of the Schedule, after paragraph 16 insert—

> **16A.** The offence under section 1 of the Theft Act 1968[^f00003], but only if it is the prosecutor’s case that the offence constitutes low-value shoplifting within the meaning of section 22A(3) of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980[^f00004].

.

Dominic Grieve

Attorney General

12th May 2014

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Order)

Section 3 of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 sets out the functions of the Director of Public Prosecutions. These include taking over the conduct of all criminal proceedings instituted on behalf of a police force, unless the proceedings are specified in an Order made by the Attorney General under section 3(3). The Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 (Specified Proceedings) Order 1999 (“the 1999 Order”) specifies certain such proceedings.

Article 2 of this Order amends the 1999 Order by adding a further offence, proceedings for which will be specified proceedings. That offence is the offence of theft, contrary to section 1 of the Theft Act 1968, in the circumstances where it is the prosecutor’s case that the offence constitutes “low-value shoplifting” within the meaning of section 22A of the Magistrates’ Courts Act 1980.
