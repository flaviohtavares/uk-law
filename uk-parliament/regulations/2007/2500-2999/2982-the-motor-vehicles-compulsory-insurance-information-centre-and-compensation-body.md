# The Motor Vehicles (Compulsory Insurance) (Information Centre and Compensation Body) (Amendment) Regulations 2007

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

This Statutory Instrument has been made in consequence of a defect in [SI 2003/37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/37) and is being issued free of charge to all known recipients of that Statutory Instrument.

The Secretary of State for Transport makes the following Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by section 2(2) of the European Communities Act 1972[^f00001].

The Secretary of State for Transport is a Minister designated for the purposes of section 2(2) of the European Communities Act 1972 in relation to compulsory insurance in respect of, and other means of providing for, civil liability in relation to motor vehicles and trailers[^f00002].

## 1. Citation and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Motor Vehicles (Compulsory Insurance) (Information Centre and Compensation Body) (Amendment) Regulations 2007 and shall come into force on 1<sup>st</sup> December 2007.

## 2. Amendment of the Motor Vehicles (Compulsory Insurance) (Information Centre and Compensation Body) Regulations 2003

(1) The Motor Vehicles (Compulsory Insurance) (Information Centre and Compensation Body) Regulations 2003[^f00003] are amended as follows.

(2) In regulation 2(1), omit the definition of “guarantee fund”.

Signed by authority of the Secretary of State for Transport

Jim Fitzpatrick

Parliamentary Under Secretary of State

Department for Transport

17th October 2007

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the Motor Vehicles (Compulsory Insurance) (Information Centre and Compensation Body) Regulations 2003 by omitting the definition of “guarantee fund”, an expression which is not used elsewhere in the Regulations, from regulation 2(1). A full regulatory impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no impact on the private or voluntary sectors is foreseen.
