<tr><td>1</td><td>Doors, their latches and hinges</td><td>1. All doors, provided on the vehicle for the entry and exit of occupants, shall be so designed as to allow easy and safe use. Doors shall have both intermediate and fully latched positions and their latches shall be designed to prevent the doors from opening accidentally. However, latching in an intermediate position is not required if a safety-bolt with an audible warning device is provided as specified in paragraph 2(b) below.<br>2. Where hinges are used, the hinges shall be fitted at the front edge of the door. However, they may be fitted at the top or rear edge if either—<br>(a) a device is fitted which automatically closes the door at least to the intermediate latched position if it is unlatched when the vehicle is moving in the forward direction; or<br>(b) a safety-bolt is fitted to prevent such unlatching together with an audible warning device which activates if the vehicle is driven without the bolt in place.<br>3. On hinged doors, the latch and striker assembly shall be capable of withstanding any load which is likely to be applied in normal driving (i.e. excluding crash impacts) in both the intermediate and fully latched positions. However, if a safety-bolt with an audible warning device is provided as specified in paragraph 2(b) above, the requirement need be met only with the bolt in place.</td><td>For the purposes of this item, “longitudinal plane” has the meaning given in regulation 3(2) of the Construction and Use Regulations.</td><td>Paragraph 3 shall not apply to doors which do not give direct access to a seat designed for normal use while a vehicle is travelling on a road. This includes any door, for which the <strong>longitudinal plane</strong> passing through the extreme inboard projecting point of the door is more than 300 mm distant from the <strong>longitudinal plane</strong> passing through the nearest edge of such a seat.</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td>Radio-interference suppression</td><td>1. The vehicle shall comply with the design, construction and fitting requirements of paragraph 6.1 of Annex 1 to Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1972/0245">72/245/EEC</a> as last amended by Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1989/0491">89/491/EEC</a>, or of paragraph 6.1 of ECE Regulation 10.01.</td><td></td><td>This item shall not apply to vehicles propelled otherwise than by a spark ignition engine.</td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td></td><td>2. The suppression equipment components shall comply with the marking requirements of paragraph 4 of Annex 1 to Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1972/0245">72/245/EEC</a> as last amended by Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1989/0491">89/491/EEC</a>, or of paragraph 4 of ECE Regulation 10.01.</td><td></td><td>Paragraph 2 shall not apply to suppression equipment fitted to vehicles which meet the requirements of either paragraph 2 or paragraph 3 of Schedule 1 to these Regulations.</td></tr>
<tr><td>3</td><td>Protective Steering</td><td>1. No part of the <strong>steering control</strong> surface directed towards the driver which can be contacted by a sphere of 165 mm in diameter shall present any roughness or sharp edges with a radius of curvature of less than 2.5 mm.<br>2. The <strong>steering control</strong> shall be designed, constructed and fitted so as not to embody components or accessories, including the horn control and other assemblies, capable of catching in the driver’s clothing or jewellery in normal driving movements.<br>3. The <strong>steering control</strong> and column assembly as fitted to the vehicle or the <strong>steering control</strong> itself shall offer a degree of protection to the driver in the event of a frontal impact by absorbing energy. This requirement shall be treated as met if either—<br>(a) the vehicle complies with the requirements of paragraph 5.2 of Annex I of Directive 74/297lEEC; or<br>(b) the <strong>steering control</strong> complies with the requirements of paragraphs 5.2, 5.4.3, 5.4.4 and 5.4.5 of Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1974/0297">74/297/EEC</a> as last amended by Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1991/0662">91/662/EEC</a>.<br>4. The vehicle shall comply with the requirements of paragraph 5.1 of Annex I of Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1974/0297">74/297/EEC</a>.</td><td>For the purposes of this item, “steering control” has the same meaning as in Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1974/0297">74/297/EEC</a>.</td><td>This item shall not apply to—<br>(a) vehicles, the <strong>steering control</strong> of which has been specially constructed for the use of a person suffering from some physical defect or disability;<br>(b) vehicles in which the centre of the <strong>steering control</strong> is in the forward quarter of the total length of the vehicle, including any bumpers and overriders; and<br>(c) motor ambulances or motor caravans (in each case) having a maximum gross weight of not less than 1500 kg.<br>Paragraph 3 shall not apply to transitional provision vehicles which have been constructed from a kit which—<br>(a) included a chassis, or a body; and<br>(b) was sold by retail before 1st January 1998.</td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td>Exhaust emissions</td><td>1. The vehicle shall not emit any avoidable smoke or avoidable visible vapour.<br>2. When the engine by which it is propelled is running without load at a <strong>normal idling speed,</strong> the carbon monoxide content of the exhaust emissions from the engine shall not exceed—<br>(a) if the vehicle has an <strong>effective date</strong> before 1st August 1986, 4.5%; or<br>(b) if the vehicle has an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1st August 1986 and is not a vehicle to which sub-paragraph (c) or (d) below applies, 3.5%;<br>(c) if the vehicle is of a description mentioned in the Annex to <strong>the emissions publication</strong> and is either—<br>(i) a passenger car having an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1st August 1992; or<br>(ii) any other vehicle having four or more wheels and an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1st August 1994,<br><strong>the relevant percentage;</strong> or<br>(d) if the vehicle is not of a description mentioned in the Annex to <strong>the emissions publication</strong> and is a <strong>passenger car</strong> having an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1st August 1994, 0.5%;<br>of the total exhaust emissions from the engine by volume.<br>3. If the engine by which the vehicle is propelled is run without load at a rotational speed of 2000 revolutions per minute, the hydrocarbon content of the exhaust emission from the engine shall not exceed 0.12% if the vehicle either—<br>(a) has not more than three wheels; or of a vehicle of a description mentioned in<br>(b) is a vehicle to which paragraph 2(a) or (b) the Annex to the emissions publication, the applies;<br>4. If the vehicle is one to which paragraph 2(c) or (d) applies and the engine by which it is propelled is run without load at <strong>a fast idling speed</strong>—<br>(a) the carbon monoxide content of the exhaust emissions from the engine shall not exceed 0.3% of the total exhaust emissions from the engine by volume;<br>(b) the hydrocarbon content of the exhaust emissions from the engine shall not exceed 0.02% of the total exhaust emissions from the engine by volume; and<br>(c) <strong>the lambda value</strong> shall be between <strong>the relevant limits.</strong></td><td>For the purposes of this item—<br>“effective date” means the date of manufacture of the vehicle, except that in the case of a vehicle which for the time being meets the requirements of paragraph 3 of Schedule 1 to these Regulations, it shall mean the 1st January immediately preceding the date of manufacture of the engine by which it is propelled, if that date is earlier;<br>“a normal idling speed” means in the case of a vehicle of a description mentioned in the Annex to <strong>the emissions publication,</strong> a rotational speed between the minimum and maximum limits shown against that description of vehicle in columns 2(b) and 2(c) respectively of that Annex;<br>“the emissions publication” is the publication entitled “In-service Exhaust Emissions Standards for Road Vehicles” (ISBN O-9526457-O-X) published by the Department of Transport;<br>“passenger car” means a motor vehicle which—<br>(a) is constructed or adapted for use for the carriage of passengers and is not a goods vehicle;<br>(b) has no more than five seats in addition to the driver’s seat.<br>(c) has a weight not exceeding 2,500 kg maximum gross; and<br>(d) has four or more wheels;<br>“the relevant percentage” means in the case percentage shown against that description of vehicle in column 2(a) of that Annex;<br>“rotary piston engine” means an engine in which the torque is provided by means of one or more rotary pistons and not by any reciprocating piston; and any rotary piston engine shall be deemed to be a four-stroke engine;<br>“a fast idling speed” means—<br>(a) in the case of a vehicle of a description mentioned in the Annex to the <strong>emissions publication,</strong> a rotational speed between the minimum and maximum limits shown against that description of vehicle in columns 3(e) and (f) respectively of that Annex; or<br>(b) in any other case, a rotational speed of between 2500 and 3000 revolutions per minute;<br>“the lambda value” means the ratio by mass, of air to petrol vapour in the mixture entering the combustion chambers divided by 14.7; and<br>“the relevant limits” mean—<br>(a) in the case of a vehicle of a description mentioned in the Annex to <strong>the emissions publication,</strong> the minimum and maximum limits shown against that description of vehicle in columns 3(c) and 3(d) respectively of that Annex; or<br>(b) in any other case, 0.97 and 1.03.</td><td>Paragraphs 2, 3 and 4 shall not apply to—<br>(a) vehicles propelled otherwise than by a spark ignition engine;<br>(b) vehicles having an effective date before 1st August 1975; and<br>(c) vehicles having an effective date before 1st August 1987 and propelled by a rotary piston engine.<br>Paragraphs 3 and 4(b) shall not apply to vehicles fuelled by natural gas.<br>Paragraph 4(c) shall not apply to vehicles or fuelled by liquid petroleum gas or natural gas.<br>Paragraphs 2(a) and (b) and 3 shall not apply to vehicles propelled by a 2-stroke engine.<br>Paragraphs 2(c) and (d) and 4 shall not apply to transitional provision vehicles which have been constructed from a kit which—<br>(a) included a chassis or a body; and<br>(b) was sold by retail before 1st January 1998.</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td>Smoke emissions</td><td>If the vehicle has an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1 August 1979, when the engine by which it is propelled is subject to the <strong>free-acceleration</strong> test, the <strong>coefficient of absorption</strong> of the exhaust emissions from the engine immediately after leaving the exhaust shall not exceed—<br>(a) if the engine of the vehicle is turbo-charged, 3.0 per metre, or<br>(b) in any other case, 2.5 per metre.</td><td>For the purposes of this item—<br>“effective date” has the same meaning as in item 4;<br>“free acceleration” has the same meaning as in Annex II to Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1977/0143">77/143/EEC</a> as last amended by Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1992/0055">92/55/EEC</a>; and .<br>“co-efficient of absorption” shall be construed in accordance with paragraph 3.5 of Annex VII to Directive 72/306.</td><td>This item shall not apply to vehicles propelled otherwise than by a compression ignition engine.</td></tr>
<tr><td>6</td><td>Lamps, reflectors and devices</td><td>The construction and equipment of the vehicle shall be such that its use on a road would not constitute a contravention or a failure to comply with—<br>(a) regulations 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17 or 19 of the 1989 Regulations; or<br>(b) regulation 18 of those Regulations except in so far as it requires a vehicle to be fitted with a dim-dip device or running lamp; or<br>(c) regulation 18 or 20 of those Regulations except in so far that either regulation prevents any direction indicator fitted to a vehicle from bearing any mark which would be permitted if the vehicle were one described by paragraph 5(b) of Schedule 7; Part I (e.g. a solo motor bicycle).</td><td>For the purposes of this item, the 1989 Regulations shall have effect as if for every reference to the date on which a vehicle is first used there were substituted a reference to the date of manufacture of the vehicle.<br>For the purposes of this item—<br>“coefficient of luminous intensity” has the same meaning as in ECE Regulation 3.01;<br>“standard mark” means a mark which when applied to a lamp, reflector or device indicates compliance with the requirements of a particular instrument; and a reference to the instrument to which a standard mark relates shall be construed accordingly.</td><td>This item so far as it requires any lamp (not being a filament lamp), reflector or device to bear a particular <strong>standard mark</strong> (or one of two or more <strong>standard marks</strong>) shall not apply if it is fitted to a vehicle which either meets the requirements of paragraph 2 of Schedule 1 to these Regulations or is a transitional provision vehicle which has been constructed from a kit which—<br>(i) included a body; and<br>(ii) was sold by retail before 1 January 1998; and<br>(b) emit sufficient light to illuminate the road in front of the vehicle on both main and dipped beam.</td></tr>
<tr><td>7</td><td>Rear-view mirrors</td><td>1. The vehicle shall be fitted with at least one offside exterior mirror which meets—<br>(a) if the vehicle is right hand drive or has a central driving position, the <strong>adjustment, dimension, rear view,</strong> and <strong>vibration requirements;</strong> or<br>(b) if the vehicle is left hand drive, the <strong>dimension, rear view,</strong> and <strong>vibration requirements.</strong><br>2. It shall also be fitted with at least one interior mirror which meets the <strong>adjustment, dimension, rear view</strong> and <strong>vibration requirements</strong> unless no interior mirror could provide any view to the rear.<br>3. If no interior mirror is fitted or if no interior mirror meets the <strong>rear view requirements,</strong> a nearside exterior mirror shall be fitted which meets—<br>(a) if the vehicle is right hand drive or has a central driving position, the <strong>dimension, rear view,</strong> and <strong>vibration requirements;</strong> or<br>(b) if the vehicle is left hand drive, the <strong>adjustment, dimension, rear view,</strong> and <strong>vibration requirements.</strong><br>4. Each rear view mirror fitted to the vehicle shall either—<br>(a) comply with the requirements (including marking requirements) of Annex II to Directive <a href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1971/0127">71/127/EEC</a> as last amended by Directive 88/321/BEG or of Part I of ECE Regulation 46.01 for a Class I mirror in the case of an interior mirror or a Class III mirror in the case of an exterior mirror; or<br>(b) comply with the following requirements—<br>(i) the edge of the reflective surface shall be enclosed within a protective housing;<br>(ii) all parts of the external surface of the mirror which are contactable, in the case of an interior mirror, by a sphere of 165 mm diameter and in the case of an exterior mirror, by a sphere of 100 mm diameter, shall have a radius of curvature of not less than 2.5 mm, with the exception of fixing holes or recesses less than 12 mm wide if these are blunted;<br>(iii) exterior mirrors shall be capable of rotating inwards towards the vehicle when a force of 10 kg is applied horizontally at the <strong>extreme outer edge</strong> in a <strong>longitudinal plane</strong> from both the front and the rear. The axis of rotation shall lie within 50 mm of the surface at which the mirror is attached to the vehicle bodywork.</td><td>For the purposes of this item—<br>“extreme outer edge” has the meaning given by regulation 3(2) of the Road Vehicles Lighting Regulations 1989;<br>“longitudinal plane” has the meaning given in regulation 3(2) of the Construction and Use Regulations;<br>“transverse plane” means a vertical plane normal to a longitudinal plane;<br>“transverse eye plane” means the vertical plane passing through the line shown as such in the Appendix to this Schedule.<br>“longitudinal plane a”, “longitudinal plane b” and “longitudinal plane c” mean the vertical planes “a”, “b” and “c” in the Appendix to this Schedule;<br>“floor marking A”, “floor marking B” and “floor marking C” mean the lines shown as such in the Appendix to this Schedule.<br>“pole A”, “pole B” and “pole C” mean the vertical lines projecting upwards from the horizontal surface (floor) at the positions shown in the Appendix to this Schedule, to the height of—<br>(a) in the case of <strong>pole A,</strong> the highest point of the reflecting surface of the offside exterior mirror;<br>(b) in the case of <strong>pole B,</strong> the highest point of the reflecting surface of the interior mirror; and<br>(c) in the case of <strong>pole C,</strong> the highest point of the reflecting surface of the nearside exterior mirror.<br><strong><em>“The adjustment requirements”</em></strong><br>In order to meet the adjustment requirements, an interior mirror shall be capable of being adjusted by the driver when in his driving position.<br>In order to meet the adjustment requirements, an exterior mirror must—<br>(a) be capable of being adjusted by the driver when in his driving position, the door being closed; or<br>(b) be so constructed that when knocked out of alignment it can be returned to its former position without need of adjustment.<br>A mirror shall be regarded as meeting the requirements of sub-paragraph (a) above whether or not—<br>(a) it can meet those requirements only with the window in the driver’s door being open; or<br>(b) the mirror is capable of being locked in position from the outside of the vehicle.<br>“<em>The dimension requirements</em>”<br>In order for an exterior mirror to meet the dimension requirements, the dimensions of the reflecting surface must be such that an orthogonal projection onto a vertical surface accommodates a vertical line with a length of 70 mm and a rectangle with vertical sides of 40 mm and horizontal sides of 70 mm.<br>In order for an interior mirror to meet the dimension requirements, the dimensions of the reflecting surface must be such that an orthogonal projection onto a vertical surface accommodates a rectangle with vertical sides of 40 mm and horizontal sides of 80 mm.<br><strong><em>“The rear view requirements” (see the Appendix to this Schedule)</em></strong><br>In order for a mirror to meet the rear view requirements, it must be such that when the vehicle is positioned such that its longitudinal axis is parallel to the <strong>longitudinal planes a, b and c,</strong> with the driver seated in his of an offside exterior mirror with the offside <strong>extreme outer edge</strong> of the vehicle positioned so that it is tangential to the <strong>longitudinal plane a,</strong> the whole of <strong>floor marking A</strong> and <strong>pole A;</strong> and<br>(b) in the case of an interior mirror, with the central longitudinal axis of the vehicle positioned so that it lies on the <strong>longitudinal plane b,</strong> the whole of <strong>floor marking B</strong> and <strong>pole B;</strong> or<br>(c) in the case of a nearside exterior mirror, with the offside <strong>extreme C.</strong><br>“<em>The vibration requirements</em>”<br>In order to comply with the vibration requirement, a mirror shall be fixed to the vehicle in such a way that under normal driving conditions, it neither moves so as to change the field of vision, nor vibrates to an extent which would cause the driver to misinterpret the nature of the image received.</td><td>The provision in column 4 of this item interpreting the <strong>dimension requirements</strong> shall have effect in relation to an exterior mirror fitted to a motor ambulance or motor caravan, as if—<br>(a) for “70 mm” in the first ambulances or motor caravans (in each case) having a maximum gross weight exceeding 3500 kg.<br>An exterior mirror fitted to a motor ambulance or motor caravan shall be treated as complying with the href="https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1988/0321">88/321/EEC</a> or of Part I of ECE Regulation 46.01 of the instruments there mentioned for a Class II mirror<br>Paragraph 4(b)(iii) shall not apply—<br>(a) to any mirror, no part of which is less than 2000 mm above the ground, or<br>(b) to any mirror which does not protrude beyond an <strong>extreme outer edge</strong> of the <tr><td>8</td><td>Anti-theft devices</td><td>1. The vehicle shall be fitted with some form of anti-theft device, that is to say, a mechanical, electrical or electronic immobiliser or a combination of these, (in addition to any key operated switch normally used to start the engine) that prevents the vehicle being driven or moved under its own power.<br>2. No anti-theft device shall operate on the braking system.<br>3. If any anti-theft device is, or incorporates, any mechanical device that acts upon a system that affects the control of the vehicle (e.g. the steering control)—<br>(a) it shall not be possible to start the engine in the normal way until every such mechanical device has been deactivated,<br>(b) it shall not be possible to activate any such mechanical device while the engine is running, and<br>(c) the action of activating any such mechanical device shall be distinct and separate from that of stopping the engine.</td><td></td><td>This item shall not apply to vehicles specially constructed (and not merely adapted) for the use of a person suffering from some physical defect or disability.</td></tr>
<tr><td>9</td><td>Seat belts</td><td>1. Every <strong>adult belt</strong> fitted to the vehicle, not being a <strong>harness belt</strong> or a <strong>passive belt,</strong> shall comply with the requirements of paragraph (7) of regulation 47 of Construction and Use Regulations to the extent (if any) that it would apply were the said paragraph (7) to apply to all motor vehicles.<br>2. <tr><td>10</td><td>Seat belt anchorages</td><td>1. The vehicle shall comply with the requirements of paragraph 4.3 of Annex I to Directive 76/l 15/EEC.<br>2. Every anchorage intended for an <strong>adult belt</strong> shall comply with the requirements of paragraphs 4.2, 4.4 and 4.5 of Annex I to Directive 76/l 15/EEC.<br>4. The vehicle shall comply with the requirements of paragraph 5 of Annex I to Directive 76/l 15/EEC.</td><td>For the Purposes of this item—<br>“disabled person’s belt”, “forward-facing seat” and “seat belt” have the same meanings as in regulation 47 of the Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) Regulations;<br>“adult belt”, “harness belt” and “passive belt” have the same meanings as in item 9.<br>For the purposes of this item, Annex I to Directive 76/l 1 S/EEC Directive shall have effect as if for paragraph 4.4.1 there were substituted—<br>4.4.1 Every belt anchorage shall comply with the following requirements.</td><td>Paragraph 3 shall not apply to anchorages which are so located that, when used with an <strong>adult belt</strong> of a type for which they are intended to be used, they provide a level of safety equivalent to that provided by anchorages that meet the requirements of that paragraph when used with an <strong>adult belt</strong> of a type for which they are intended.</td></tr>
<tr><td>11</td><td>Installation of seat belts</td><td>1. The vehicle shall be fitted with, a <strong>harness belt,</strong> a <strong>passive belt</strong> or a <strong>three point belt</strong> for the driver’s seat and the <strong>specified passenger seat</strong> (if any).<br>2. If the vehicle has an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1st April 1987, in addition to the <strong>seat belts</strong> required by paragraph 1 above, it shall be fitted with an <strong>appropriate belt</strong> for every other <strong>forward-facing seat</strong> in the front alongside the driver’s seat, not being a <strong>specified passenger’s seat.</strong><br>3. If the vehicle has an <strong>effective date</strong> on or after 1st April 1987, in addition to the seat belts required by paragraph 1 and 2 above, it shall be fitted with a <strong>harness belt,</strong> a <strong>lap belt,</strong> a <strong>passive belt</strong> or <strong>threepoint belt</strong> for every other <strong>forward-facing seat.</strong><br>4. Every <strong>adult belt</strong> and every <strong>disabled person’s belt</strong> fitted to the vehicle shall be properly secured to anchorages provided for it and installed so that it can be used properly to restrain an occupant (e.g. in the case of a lap belt by passing over the pelvic region and in the case of a <strong>harness belt,</strong> a <strong>passive belt</strong> or a <strong>three-point belt</strong> by also restraining the upper torso and not being liable to slip off the shoulder). In particular, under all normal conditions likely to be encountered while the vehicle is being driven on a road, including when the vehicle is loaded to its maximum gross weight and the axle weight of any one axle equals its maximum permitted axle weight—<br>(a) it shall be possible to control the vehicle safely, taking account of the vehicle’s speed and acceleration capabilities;<br>(b) it shall at all times be possible for the driver, while controlling the vehicle, to have a full view of the road and traffic ahead of the vehicle;<br>(c) the clearance between any of the following parts of the vehicle, namely the tyres, wheels, other rotating components associated with the transmission of engine power or braking, moveable parts of the steering mechanism and fixed parts shall be sufficient to ensure no risk of fouling and the wheels and wheel-fixings shall be compatible;<br>(d) the vehicle structure (chassis or integral chassis-body), shall be so designed and constructed as to withstand the forces and vibration to which it is likely to be subject;<br>(e) all entries and exits provided for the vehicle occupants (other than doors) shall be so designed as to allow easy and safe use;<br>(f) ail aperture covers (other than doors) including tail gates, boot and bonnet lids, shall be capable of being securely latched in the fully closed position;<br>(g) the steering, suspension, axles and wheels shall be so designed, constructed and fitted as to withstand the forces and vibration to which they are likely to be subject;<br>(h) the fuel system, including the fuel tank, shall be so designed, constructed and fitted as to withstand the forces, vibration and corrosive environment to which it is likely to be subject; it shall also be so fitted as to avoid the risk of damage, such as abrasion, due to fouling of other parts and to minimise the risk of fire in the event of any leakage of fuel; the fuel tank shall comply with regulation 39 of the Construction and Use Regulations, except that it need not be made of metal;<br>(i) the electrical system, including the battery, electrical components and wires, shall be securely attached to the vehicle. In particular, the maximum distance between attachments of wires, except those contained within hollow components, shall be 300 mm and wires shall be so fitted and protected as to avoid the risk of damage to insulation, such as abrasion. All components shall be so constructed and fitted as to minimise the risk of corrosion and fire.</td></tr>
