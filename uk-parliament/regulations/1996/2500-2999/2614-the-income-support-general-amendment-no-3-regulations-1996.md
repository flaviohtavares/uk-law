# The Income Support (General) Amendment (No. 3) Regulations 1996

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

1996 No. 2614

SOCIAL SECURITY

The Income Support (General) Amendment (No. 3) Regulations 1996

Made

14th October 1996

Laid before Parliament

18th October 1996

Coming into force

8th November 1996

The Secretary of State for Social Security, in exercise of the powers conferred on him by sections 135(1), 137(1) and 175(1) and (3) of the Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992[^f00001], and of all other powers enabling him in that behalf, after agreement by the Social Security Advisory Committee that proposals in respect of these Regulations should not be referred to it[^f00002], hereby makes the following Regulations:

## 1. Citation and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Income Support (General) Amendment (No. 3) Regulations 1996 and shall come into force on 8th November 1996.

## 2. Amendment of regulation 21 of the Income Support (General) Regulations 1987

In paragraph (3A) of regulation 21 of the Income Support (General) Regulations 1987[^f00003] (applicable amounts for persons in residential accommodation), the words from “and the local authority” to the end of the paragraph shall be omitted.

Signed by authority of the Secretary of State for Social Security.

Roger Evans

Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State,

Department of Social Security

14th October 1996

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the Income Support (General) Regulations 1987 (S.I. [1987/1967](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/1967)). In particular, regulation 2 amends those Regulations so as to provide that a person who is in residential accommodation provided by a local authority and who is still in such accommodation when that accommodation becomes a residential care home for the purpose of those Regulations, will be treated as being in residential accommodation, notwithstanding that the local authority may no longer be under a duty to provide or make arrangements for providing accommodation for that person.

These Regulations do not impose a charge on business.
