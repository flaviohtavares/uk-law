# The Merchant Shipping (Fees) Regulations 1991

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

1991 No. 784

MERCHANT SHIPPING

The Merchant Shipping (Fees) Regulations 1991

Made

18th March 1991

Laid before Parliament

25th March 1991

Coming into force

15th April 1991

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred by:—

- (i) section 5(3) of the Merchant Shipping Act 1948[^f00001],
- (ii) section 33 of the Merchant Shipping (Safety Convention) Act 1949[^f00002],
- (iii) section 26 of the Merchant Shipping (Load Lines) Act 1967[^f00003],
- (iv) section 1(1)(d) of the Anchors and Chain Cables Act 1967[^f00004],
- (v) section 6 of the Fishing Vessels (Safety Provisions) Act 1970[^f00005],
- (vi) section 84 of the Merchant Shipping Act 1970[^f00006],
- (vii) section 17 of and Schedule 5 to the Merchant Shipping Act 1974[^f00007],
- (viii) section 21(1) and (3)(r) of the Merchant Shipping Act 1979[^f00008],
- (ix) section 5(1) and (8) of, and paragraph 2(j) of the Schedule, to the Merchant Shipping Act 1983[^f00009],
- (x) article 3(1)(a) of the Merchant Shipping (Prevention of Oil Pollution) Order 1983[^f00010],
- (xi) article 3(2)(a) of the Merchant Shipping (Prevention and Control of Oil Pollution) Order 1987[^f00011],
- (xii) section 13(1) and (7) of, and paragraph 2(i) of Schedule 2 to, the Merchant Shipping Act 1988[^f00012],

and now vested in him and all other powers enabling him in that behalf[^f00013], after consulting with the persons referred to in section 22(2) of the Act of 1979 and with the consent and approval of the Treasury (except in respect of the powers conferred by the Acts of 1948 and 1974) hereby makes the following Regulations:

**1.** These Regulations may be cited as the Merchant Shipping (Fees) Regulations 1991 and shall come into force on 15th April 1991.

**2.** (1) In these Regulations:—

- “the Act of 1948” means the Merchant Shipping Act 1948;
- “the Act of 1949” means the Merchant Shipping (Safety Convention) Act 1949;
- “the Act of 1967” means the Merchant Shipping (Load Lines) Act 1967;
- “the Act of 1970” means the Merchant Shipping Act 1970;
- “the Act of 1974” means the Merchant Shipping Act 1974;
- “the Act of 1979” means the Merchant Shipping Act 1979;
- “the principal Act” means the Merchant Shipping Act 1894[^f00014]; and
- “tons” means gross tons and the gross tonnage of a ship having alternative gross tonnages shall be taken to be the larger of those tonnages.

(2) Subject to regulation 3 below the following Regulations are hereby revoked:—

- The Merchant Shipping (Fees) Regulations 1990[^f00015]
- The Merchant Shipping (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations 1990[^f00016].

**3.** Nothing in these Regulations shall apply to any work involved in carrying out a service before these Regulations come into force.

**4.** (1) Where a fee is determined by the amount of work involved on or off the ship—

- (a) travelling time in excess of 4 hours for each visit to a ship in the United Kingdom shall be disregarded except for the initial survey or inspection of a fishing vessel under the Fishing Vessels (Safety Provisions) Rules 1975[^f00017] or the Merchant Shipping (Crew Accommodation) (Fishing Vessels) Regulations 1975[^f00018];
- (b) travelling time includes the time taken to travel from the United Kingdom to a ship overseas subject to a maximum of 10 hours in any 24 hour period;
- (c) travelling time in respect of the survey of a fishing vessel for the renewal of a United Kingdom fishing vessel certificate under the Fishing Vessels (Safety Provisions) Rules 1975 shall be subject to a minimum charge of £44 for each visit required for the survey;
- (d) the cost of travelling and subsistence incurred in visiting a ship outside the United Kingdom shall be charged additionally to the hourly rate;
- (e) where the work is carried out in conjunction with a non-statutory survey for which fees are not payable under these Regulations, the work involved shall be the extra time taken to ensure compliance with the requirements for which fees are charged under these Regulations;
- (f) ny specific costs incurred in respect of computer or outside services shall be charged additionally to the hourly rate.

(2) The cost of travelling and subsistence in respect of tonnage measurement incurred in visiting a ship outside the United Kingdom shall be chargeable additionally to the fee in paragraphs 3 and 4 of Part II of the Schedule.

**5.** (1) The fees payable for the services specified in the Schedule to these Regulations shall be the fees specified in relation thereto in that Schedule.

(2) The fee in the case of a survey or periodical inspection for the issue, renewal or endorsement of a certificate shall cover the issue or endorsement of that certificate.

(3) Where a fee payable under these Regulations is for, or covers, the issue or endorsement of a certificate, the certificate need not be issued or endorsed unless that fee has been paid.

(4) If the duration of the work actually required in consequence of the application is greater or less than its estimated duration, the fee shall be recalculated by reference to the actual duration and the difference shall be paid by, or, as the case may be, refunded to, the applicant.

### PART II — tonnage measurement

#### 1. Fees for Measurement of Ship’s Tonnage

The fees prescribed in this Part include the survey and certification under section 6 and the inspection of markings under section 7(1) of the principal Act.

**2.** In this Part:—

- “the Regulations” means the Merchant Shipping (Tonnage) Regulations 1982[^f00020] :
- “sister ship” means a ship which corresponds in all major respects relevant to the measurement for tonnage with a ship the tonnage of which has been previously ascertained under the Regulations, and for which the calculation for tonnage is available to the Certifying Authority to which, prior to the commencement of its measurement for tonnage, application has been made in writing by or on behalf of the owner of the ship requesting it to be treated as a sister ship.

These Regulations revoke the Merchant Shipping (Fees) Regulations 1990 and the Merchant Shipping (Fees) (Amendment) Regulations 1990.

The Regulations prescribe changes in some of the rates charged by the Department of Transport and Customs and Excise. Fees for fishing vessel surveys have been reduced by 11%, but all associated office hours are to be charged instead of a flat rate for five hours. The fee previously claimed for wreck taken by the Receiver into his custody has been waived and deleted from the Regulations. Customs and Excise fees have been increased by 25—50% Fees will also in future be charged for the issue of a Licence for Marine Engineer Operators and for the Certificate of Service (Fishing Vessels) (Deck or Engineer). All other increases are in line with inflation.
