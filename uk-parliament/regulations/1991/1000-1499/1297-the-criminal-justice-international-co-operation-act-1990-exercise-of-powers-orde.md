# The Criminal Justice (International Co-operation) Act 1990 (Exercise of Powers) Order 1991

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

1991 No. 1297

CUSTOMS AND EXCISE

The Criminal Justice (International Co-operation) Act 1990 (Exerciseof Powers) Order 1991

Made

4th June 1991

Laid before Parliament

7th June 1991

Coming into force

1st July 1991

The Treasury, in exercise of the powers conferred on them bysections 7(7) and 8(5) of the Criminal Justice (InternationalCo-operation) Act 1990[^f00001], hereby make the following Order:

**1.** This Order may be cited as the Criminal Justice (InternationalCo-operation) Act 1990 (Exercise of Powers) Order 1991 and shall comeinto operation on 1st July 1991.

**2.** In this Order—

- “the Act” means the Criminal Justice (International Co-operation) Act 1990;
- “officer” means a person commissioned by the Commissioners of Customs andExcise under section 6(3) of the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979[^f00002].

**3.** In England and Wales and Northern Ireland, any powers exercisable bya constable under section 7 of the Act, shall also be exercisable by anofficer or by any person acting under the direction of an officer.

**4.** In Scotland, any powers to enter, search or seize granted undersection 8(1) of the Act shall also be exercisable by an officer or byany person acting under the direction of an officer.

Sydney Chapman

Irvine Patnick

Two of the Lords Commissioners of Her Majesty’s Treasury

4th June 1991

(This is not part of the Order)

Articles 3 and 4 of the Order apply to officers of Customs andExcise the provisions of sections 7 and 8 of the Criminal Justice(International Co-operation) Act 1990. Those provisions relate to powersof entry, search and seizure exercisable by the police for the purposeof obtaining evidence relevant to overseas investigations.
