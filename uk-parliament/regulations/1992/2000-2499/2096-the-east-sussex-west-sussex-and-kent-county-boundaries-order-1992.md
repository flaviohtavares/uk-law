# The East Sussex, West Sussex and Kent (County Boundaries) Order 1992

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

Made

for the purposes referred to in article 1(2)

1st October 1992

The Secretary of State, in exercise of his powers under section 51(2) and 67(4) of the Local Government Act 1972[^f00001] and of all other powers enabling him in that behalf, hereby makes the following Order which gives effect with modifications to proposals submitted to him under sections 47, 48(1) and 51(1) of that Act, more than six weeks before the making of this Order by the Local Government Boundary Commission for England:

## 1. Citation and commencement

(1) This Order may be cited as the East Sussex, West Sussex and Kent (County Boundaries) Order 1992.

(2) This Order shall come into force on 1st October 1992 for any purpose set out in regulation 4(1) of the Local Government Area Changes Regulations 1976[^f00002].

(3) For all other purposes, this Order shall come into force on 1st April 1993.

## 2. Interpretation

In this Order—

- “district” includes a borough;
- “East Sussex” means the county of East Sussex, and any reference to Brighton, to Hove, to Lewes, to Rother or to Wealden is a reference to the East Sussex district of that name;
- “electoral division” means an electoral division of the county to which an area is transferred;
- “Kent” means the county of Kent, and any reference to Tunbridge Wells is a reference to the Kent district of that name;
- “the map” means one of the 16 maps prepared by the Department of the Environment, marked “Maps of the East Sussex, West Sussex and Kent (County Boundaries) Order 1992”, duly numbered for reference, and deposited in accordance with regulation 5 of the Local Government Area Changes Regulations 1976;
- any reference to an area as unparished is a reference to that area not forming part of any parish;
- any reference to a ward is a reference to a ward of the district to or from which an area is transferred, except in the expression “parish ward”, where it means a ward of the parish to which an area is transferred; and
- “West Sussex” means the county of West Sussex, and any reference to Adur or to Mid Sussex is a reference to the West Sussex district of that name.

## 9. Town and country planning

(1) In this article, “provision” means a provision of a development plan as defined in section 336 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990[^f00003].

(2) Any provision applying to an area transferred by this Order shall continue to apply to that area, notwithstanding the transfer, until replaced by a provision which so applies, and which is expressed to supersede the preserved provision.

Signed by authority of the Secretary of State for the Environment

Minister of State,

Department of the Environment

31st August 1992

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order transfers areas between—

- (a) the county of East Sussex (affecting the boundaries of Brighton, Hove, Lewes, Rother and Wealden) and the county of West Sussex (affecting those of Adur and Mid Sussex); and
- (b) the county of East Sussex (Rother and Wealden) and the county of Kent (Tunbridge Wells).

Consequential changes are made to the boundaries of parishes, local government electoral areas, petty sessional divisions and coroner’s districts.

The map and table at the end of this note (pages 6 and 7) give a general guide to the areas affected. The table also gives an approximate figure for the total area affected by each set of transfers, and the population affected. Prints of the boundary maps (described in article 2), which give full details of the transfers effected, may be inspected at the offices of the council for each of the boroughs and districts whose area is altered (namely Adur, Brighton, Hove, Lewes, Mid Sussex, Rother, Tunbridge Wells, and Wealden) and at the offices of the Secretary of State for the Environment located in 2 Marsham Street, London SW1P 3EB.

The Local Government Area Changes Regulations 1976 contain general provisions regarding the effect of orders such as the present one.
