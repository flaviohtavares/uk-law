# The Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Amendment and Revocation) Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2026 No. 607

RETAINED EU LAW REFORM

DANGEROUS DRUGS

The Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Amendment and Revocation) Regulations 2026

Made

8th June 2026

Coming into force

29th June 2026

The Secretary of State makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 14(2), (3), (4)(a), (b), (c) and (e) and (7) and 20(1)(a) of the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023[^f00001](“**the 2023 Act**”).

The Secretary of State is a relevant national authority[^f00002] for the purposes of section 14(2) and (3) of the 2023 Act.

In accordance with paragraph 5(1) of Schedule 5 to the 2023 Act, a draft of this instrument has been laid before Parliament and approved by a resolution of each House of Parliament.

## 1. Citation, commencement and extent

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Amendment and Revocation) Regulations 2026 and come into force 21 days after the day on which they are made.

(2) Subject to paragraphs (3) and (4), these Regulations extend to England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

(3) Regulations 5 to 8 extend to England and Wales, and Scotland only.

(4) Regulation 9 and the Schedule extend to Northern Ireland only.

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations are made in exercise of the powers in sections 14(2), (3), (4)(a), (b), (c) and (e) and (7) and 20(1)(a) of the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act [2023 (c. 28)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/28) (“**the 2023 Act**”). These Regulations revoke, replace and make alternative provision in secondary assimilated law which establish the processes and rules governing the control and trade in drug precursor chemicals in Great Britain, and trade between Great Britain, Northern Ireland and third countries.

Article 5(4) of the Windsor Framework[^f00022] provides that EU law listed in Annex 2 will apply to and in the UK in respect of Northern Ireland.  Regulation [(EC) No 273/2004](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2004/0273) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 February 2004 on drug precursors and Council Regulation [(EC) No 111/2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2005/0111) laying down rules for the monitoring of trade between the Union and third countries in drug precursors are listed in Annex 2 and therefore continue to apply in Northern Ireland as they have effect in EU law.

Regulation 2 amends the Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Intra-Community Trade) Regulations 2008 ([S.I. 2008/295](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/295)) to update references to [Regulation (EC) 273/2004](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2004/0273) as it applies in Great Britain as secondary assimilated law within the meaning of sections 5 and 11(2) of the 2023 Act and as it applies in Northern Ireland in accordance with Annex 2 of the Windsor Framework.

Regulation 3 amends the Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Community External Trade) Regulations 2008 ([S.I. 2008/296](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2008/296)) to update references to [Regulation (EC) 111/2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2005/0111) as it applies in Great Britain as secondary assimilated law within the meaning of sections 5 and 11(2) of the 2023 Act and as it applies in Northern Ireland in accordance with Annex 2 of the Windsor Framework.

Regulations 2 and 3 also clarify that the penalty provisions set out in regulation 6 of the Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Intra-Community Trade) Regulations 2008 and regulation 5 of the Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Community External Trade) Regulations 2008 apply to “scheduled substances” within the meaning of Article 2(a) of [Regulation (EC) 273/2004](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2004/0273) and Article 2(a) of Regulation [(EC) 111/2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2005/0111).

Regulation 4 amends the Controlled Drugs (Drug Precursors) (Intra-Community Trade and Community External Trade) Regulations 2010 ([S.I. 2010/2564](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2010/2564)) to update the references to Regulation [(EC) No 273/2004](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2004/0273) and [Regulation (EC) 111/2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2015/0111) and makes provision to exempt the fees that would otherwise be payable in relation to export and import authorisations required for the trade in drug precursor chemicals between Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

Regulations 5 to 8 amend deficiencies in secondary assimilated law arising from the Windsor Framework, in particular by substituting references to “the United Kingdom” with “Great Britain” to reflect the fact that the legislation applies as secondary assimilated law only in Great Britain. This ensures that the procedures under [Regulation (EC) 111/2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2005/0111) will apply to the movement of drug precursor chemicals between Great Britain and Northern Ireland. Regulations 5 and 6 also update outdated definitions and amend the annexes to [Regulation (EC) 273/2004](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2004/0273) and [Regulation (EC) 111/2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2005/0111) to control certain drug precursor chemicals in Great Britain, which brings regulated drug precursor chemicals in Great Britain in line with those controlled in Northern Ireland (by the European Union). Regulation 7 also revokes the forms in Annexes II and III to Commission Delegated [Regulation (EU) 2015/1011](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2015/1011) which are no longer used in Great Britain.

Regulation 9 revokes the instruments listed in the Schedule in so far as they extend to Northern Ireland.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no, or no significant, impact on the private, voluntary or public sector is foreseen.
