# The Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Commencement No. 1) (England) Regulations 2026

2026 No. 551 (C. 44)

EDUCATION, ENGLAND

The Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Commencement No. 1) (England) Regulations 2026

Made

20th May 2026

The Secretary of State makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by section 78(5) and (6) of the Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026[^f00001].

## 1. Citation

These Regulations may be cited as the Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Commencement No. 1) (England) Regulations 2026.

## 2. Provision coming into force on 1st June 2026

Section 33 (free school meals etc: information sharing) of the Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 comes into force on 1st June 2026.

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations are the first commencement regulations bringing into force provision in the Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act [2026 (c. 21)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21) (“**the Act**”).

Regulation 2 brings into force, on 1st June 2026, section 33 of the Act (free school meals etc: information sharing).

Section 33 of the Act amends section 110 of the Education Act [2005 (c. 18)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/18) (“**the 2005 Act**”), which makes provision for social security and tax credit information held by other government departments to be supplied to the Department for Education and the Welsh Ministers, and ultimately to local authorities in England and Wales, for use in determining eligibility of claimants for free school lunches and milk.

The amendments made by section 33 of the Act to section 110 of the 2005 Act enable social security (including asylum support) and tax credit information to be shared with the appropriate authority of certain schools in England (as well as local authorities) for the purpose of determining eligibility for free school lunches and milk. They also enable the information to be shared for the purpose of determining whether financial assistance is payable to a local authority or relevant school in England in connection with child welfare or the provision of education. They also enable a person with whom the information is shared to communicate decisions made about eligibility for free school lunches and milk, and about whether financial assistance is payable, with parents, local authorities and relevant schools in England.
