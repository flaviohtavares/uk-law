# The Marine Licensing (Exempted Activities and Fees) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

This Statutory Instrument has been made in consequence of a defect in [S.I. 2026/1110](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1110) and is being issued free of charge to all known recipients of that Statutory Instrument.

2026 No. 1180

ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION

LICENSING (MARINE)

MARINE POLLUTION

The Marine Licensing (Exempted Activities and Fees) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2026

Made

10th November 2026

Laid before Parliament

11th November 2026

Coming into force in accordance with regulation 1(1)

The Secretary of State makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 67(2) and (3), 72A(4) and (5), 74(1) and (2), 107A(3), 107B(5) and 316(1) of the Marine and Coastal Access Act 2009[^f00001].

The Secretary of State is the appropriate licensing authority under section 113(2)(a), (4)(a), (6)(a) and (8) of that Act[^f00002].

The Secretary of State has had regard to the matters mentioned in section 74(4) of that Act, and has consulted in accordance with section 74(5) of that Act, before making provision in these Regulations under section 74 of that Act.

In deciding to make provision in these Regulations under section 67(2) and 72A(4) of that Act in respect of types of application to the licensing authority or in respect of an activity of the licensing authority, the Secretary of State has decided what provision to make by reference to the expected costs of dealing with that type of application or of carrying out that activity, in accordance with section 107B(2) of that Act.

## Part 1 — Preliminary

### 1. Citation, commencement and extent

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Marine Licensing (Exempted Activities and Fees) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2026 and come into force—

- (a) as regards regulations 1 and 33, on 8th December 2026; and
- (b) otherwise on 4th January 2027.

(2) These Regulations extend to England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

## Part 4 — Transitional provision in relation to fees

### 29. Interpretation of Part 4

(1) In this Part—

- “**2014 Regulations**” means the Marine Licensing (Application Fees) Regulations 2014;
- “**amendments**” means the amendments made to the 2014 Regulations by Part 3;
- “**relevant date**” means 4th January 2027.

(2) In this Part, “**hourly rate**”, “**licence**” and “**relevant year**” have the same meaning as in the 2014 Regulations.

### 30. Transitional provision in relation to licence application fees

(1) The amendments do not apply in relation to a fee payable for determining an application for a licence where the application—

- (a) was made before the relevant date, and
- (b) fell within one of the descriptions in Band 2 in the Schedule to the 2014 Regulations, as those Regulations stood immediately before the relevant date.

(2) Subject to paragraph (1), the amendments apply on and after the relevant date in relation to a fee payable for determining an application for a licence so that—

- (a) the fee specified in the table in paragraph 1 of Schedule 1 to the 2014 Regulations (as inserted by these Regulations) is payable in relation to determining such an application where the fee is due for payment on or after the relevant date, but
- (b) hours worked or travelled before the relevant date in connection with the application are calculated at the rate of £122 per hour.

### 31. Transitional provision in relation to monitoring fees

(1) The amendments apply on and after the relevant date in relation to a fee payable for the monitoring of a licence described in column 1 of table 1 of Schedule 2 to the 2014 Regulations (as inserted by these Regulations) so that—

- (a) the fee specified in table 1 in Schedule 2 to the 2014 Regulations (as inserted by these Regulations) is payable in relation to monitoring where the fee is due for payment on or after the relevant date, but
- (b) hours worked or travelled before the relevant date in connection with the monitoring are calculated at the rate of £94 per hour.

(2) The amendments apply in relation to a fee payable for the monitoring of a licence described in column 1 of table 2 of Schedule 2 to the 2014 Regulations (as inserted by these Regulations) in respect of the relevant year beginning with 1st January 2027 and subsequent relevant years.

### 32. Transitional provision in relation to licence variation and transfer fees

(1) The amendments do not apply in relation to a fee payable for determining an application for the variation or transfer of a licence which—

- (a) was made before the relevant date, and
- (b) was, immediately before the relevant date, subject to a maximum fee specified in the table in Schedule 2 to the Public Bodies (Marine Management Organisation) (Fees) Order 2014[^f00013] of £50 or £200.

(2) Subject to paragraph (1), the amendments apply on and after the relevant date in relation to a fee payable for determining an application for the variation or transfer of a licence, or for the consideration of the variation of a licence by notice given by the licensing authority, so that—

- (a) the fee specified in Schedule 3 to the 2014 Regulations (as inserted by these Regulations) is payable in relation to determining such an application, or considering such a variation, where the fee is due for payment on or after the relevant date, but
- (b) hours worked or travelled before the relevant date in connection with the application or consideration are calculated at the rate of £94 per hour.

The Marine Licensing (Exempted Activities and Fees) (Amendment) Regulations 2026[^f00014] are revoked.

Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State

## Schedule 1 — Substitution of Schedule 1 (application bands and maximum fees)

Regulation 26

The following Schedule is substituted as Schedule 1 to the Marine Licensing (Application Fees) Regulations 2014—

> **Schedule 1 — Application bands and maximum fees**
>
> Regulation 4
>
> **1.** The bands and (where applicable) maximum fees are as follows—
>
> | Band | Description of application | Maximum fee |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | Band 1 | Any application where paragraph 2(1) applies to the activity which relates to— |  |
> |  | - (a) the burial of a body at sea;<br>- (b) erecting scaffolding for discrete maintenance projects;<br>- (c) removing poles, girders, joists and objects of a similar nature unattached to the seabed or any structure;<br>- (d) the deposit of a single scientific instrument (other than a seismic airgun), and equipment associated with such an instrument, in connection with any scientific experiment or survey, where that deposit causes or is likely to cause obstruction or danger to navigation, in particular where any such deposit is tethered to the seabed or reduces navigational clearance by more than 5% by reference to Chart Datum;<br>- (e) activities related to the detection of unexploded ordnance;<br>- (f) the removal by a local planning authority, a harbour authority or the Environment Agency, or a person acting on behalf of such an authority or agency, of a vessel which is sunk or stranded, or which has been abandoned on the sea bed, where—<br>  - (i) the vessel is, or is likely to become, a risk to the environment, safe navigation or health and safety; and<br>  - (ii) paragraph 2(2) applies in relation to the removal;<br>- (g) the deposit of fireworks from a vehicle, vessel, marine structure or floating container for the purposes of a firework display by a local planning authority or a harbour authority, or by a person acting on behalf of such an authority, where paragraph 2(3) applies in relation to the deposit;<br>- (h) resurfacing a slipway;<br>- (i) repairing joints of any structure;<br>- (j) any activity of a similar minor nature. |  |
> |  |  |  |
> | Band 2 | Any application where paragraph 2(1) applies to the activity, which does not fall, or which does not fall exclusively, within the description in Band 1, and which relates to a specified activity or activities which has, or (in the case of more than one activity) taken together have, an estimated cost ceiling within one of the following ranges— |  |
> | Band 2A | less than £20,000 | £2,950 |
> | Band 2B | £20,000 or more, but less than £75,000 | £4,650 |
> | Band 2C | £75,000 or more, but less than £250,000 | £9,290 |
> | Band 2D | £250,000 or more, but less than £1,000,000 | £12,390 |
> | Band 3 | Any other application | No maximum |
>
> **2.** (1) This paragraph applies in relation to an activity which is not—
>
> - (a) to be carried out in the course of an Annex I project;
> - (b) to be carried out in the course of an Annex II project, if it is likely because of its size, nature or location to have significant effects on the environment;
> - (c) a plan or project likely (either alone or in combination with other plans or projects) to have a significant effect on a European site;
> - (d) capable of affecting (other than insignificantly)—
>   - (i) the protected features of an MCZ; or
>   - (ii) any ecological or geomorphological process on which the conservation of any protected feature of an MCZ is (wholly or in part) dependent;
> - (e) likely to have a significant effect on a Ramsar site; or
> - (f) an activity with respect to which an environmental impact assessment is required by virtue of regulation 5 (requirement of assessment by agreement) of the EIA Regulations.
>
> (2) This paragraph applies in relation to the removal of a vessel if—
>
> - (a) the vessel is located within one nautical mile of the baseline;
> - (b) the vessel is not located within a highly protected marine area.
>
> (3) This paragraph applies in relation to the deposit of fireworks if—
>
> - (a) the deposit of the fireworks does not take place in a highly protected marine area;
> - (b) the deposit of the fireworks does not cause, and would not be likely to cause, obstruction or danger to navigation.
>
> (4) In this Schedule—
>
> - “**Annex I project**” means a project of a type specified in Annex I to [Directive 2011/92/EU](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2011/0092) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2011 on the assessment of the effects of certain public and private projects on the environment[^f00015];
> - “**Annex II project**” means a project of a type specified in Annex II to [Directive 2011/92/EU](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2011/0092) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2011 on the assessment of the effects of certain public and private projects on the environment;
> - “**EIA Regulations**” means the Marine Works (Environmental Impact Assessment) Regulations 2007[^f00016];
> - “**environmental impact assessment**” means an assessment under Part 3 of the EIA Regulations;
> - “**European site**” means a European site within the meaning of regulation 8(1) of the Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations 2017[^f00017] or a “European offshore marine site” within the meaning of regulation 18 of the Conservation of Offshore Marine Habitats and Species Regulations 2017[^f00018];
> - “**harbour authority**” has the meaning given by section 57(1) of the Harbours Act 1964[^f00019];
> - “**highly protected marine area**” means a marine conservation zone from time to time designated by an Order made under section 116 of the Act, the title of which includes “Highly Protected Marine Area”;
> - “**local planning authority**” has the same meaning as in Part 1 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990[^f00020];
> - “**MCZ**” means a marine conservation zone from time to time designated by an Order made under section 116 of the Act;
> - “**plan or project**” has the same meaning as in Council Directive [92/43/EEC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1992/0043) of 21 May 1992 on the conservation of natural habitats and of wild fauna and flora[^f00021];
> - “**protected feature**” has the meaning given by section 147(1) of the Act;
> - “**Ramsar site**” means a site described in section 37A of the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981[^f00022];
> - “**specified activity**” means any activity falling within item 1 (deposits within the UK marine licensing area etc), 7 (construction, alteration or improvement of works etc) or 8 (use of vehicle to remove substances etc) of section 66(1) of the Act.

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the Marine Licensing (Exempted Activities) Order 2011 ([S.I. 2011/409](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2011/409)) (“**the 2011 Order**”) and the Marine Licensing (Application Fees) Regulations 2014 ([S.I. 2014/615](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/615)) (“**the Fees Regulations**”).

Part 2 amends the 2011 Order, which specifies licensable marine activities in respect of which a marine licence under Part 4 of the Marine and Coastal Access Act [2009 (c. 23)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2009/23) (“**the 2009 Act**”) is not required, whether subject to conditions or otherwise. The amendments create new exemptions from the need for a marine licence in relation to maintenance of bridges or cantilevered structures and replacement of existing swinging moorings with advanced mooring systems. They also modify certain existing exemptions, including to make some of them unavailable where the exempted activity might impact a highly protected marine area.

Part 3 amends the Fees Regulations, which prescribe the fees payable in relation to marine licence applications where the Secretary of State is the appropriate licensing authority under the 2009 Act. The hourly rate payable for application fees is increased to £155, and becomes subject to annual increases in line with increases in the consumer prices index. Four new activities are added to Band 1 (which attracts an application fee of £50) in what will become Schedule 1 to the Fees Regulations (activities related to the detection of unexploded ordnance, the deposit of scientific instruments or equipment which might cause an obstruction or danger to navigation, recovery of abandoned vessels, and the deposit of fireworks). Paragraph 2 of Schedule 1 sets out matters which determine the availability of Band 1 and Band 2 licences. Sub-paragraphs (1) and (2) apply to the activities listed in Band 1 relating to the removal of a vessel and the deposit of fireworks respectively, while sub-paragraph (3) applies to any Band 1 or 2 activity. 

The amendments also provide for fees in relation to monitoring, variations and transfers of marine licences under powers introduced into the 2009 Act by section 232 of the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act [2023 (c. 55)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/55). These fees were previously provided for by the Public Bodies (Marine Management Organisation) (Fees) Order 2014 ([S.I. 2014/2555](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/2555)), which is revoked on the date these Regulations come into force by subsection (9) of that section. The new provision for these fees in the Fees Regulations largely reflects the provision that was made in that Order, but with the new hourly rate that now applies for application fees, increased maximum fees and other minor updates.

Part 4 includes transitional provision in relation to the new fees. Part 5 revokes the Marine Licensing (Exempted Activities and Fees) (Amendment) Regulations 2026 ([S.I. 2026/1110](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1110)). An error was made in the making of those Regulations and this instrument replaces them.

No impact assessment has been produced for these Regulations as no, or no significant, impact on the private, voluntary or public sector is foreseen. A de minimis assessment of the effect that this instrument will have on the cost of business has been prepared and is available from the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, 2 Marsham Street, London SW1P 4DF.
