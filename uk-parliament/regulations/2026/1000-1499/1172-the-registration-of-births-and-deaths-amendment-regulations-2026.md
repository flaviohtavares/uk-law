# The Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2026 No. 1172

REGISTRATION OF BIRTHS, DEATHS & MARRIAGES, ETC., ENGLAND AND WALES

The Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Regulations 2026

Made

4th November 2026

Coming into force

9th November 2026

The Registrar General for England and Wales makes these Regulations, with the approval of the Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 1(1), 3A(2), 5, 7(1), 9(1) and (2), 10(1) (b) to (g) and (1B) (b) to (f), 10A(1) (b) to (f) and (1B) (b) to (f), 11(1)(b), 12, 14(1), 14A(1), 15, 23(2), (2A), (2B), (2C), (3) and (4), 23A(1) and (3), 26(1), 29(2), 33(2), 33A(2), and 39 of the Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953[^f00001] as extended by [section 26](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/38/section/26) [(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/38/section/26) and [(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/38/section/26) of the [Welsh Language Act 1993](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/38/contents)[^f00002], and section 5(2) of the Foundling Hospital Act 1953[^f00003].

## 1. Citation, commencement and extent

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Regulations 2026 and come into force on 9th November 2026.

(2) These Regulations extend to England and Wales.

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the [Registration of Births and Deaths Regulations 1987](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2088/contents) ([S.I. 1987/2088](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2088)) (“**the 1987 Regulations**”) and the [Registration of Births and Deaths (Welsh Language) Regulations 1987](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2089/contents) ([S.I. 1987/2089](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2089)) (“**the Welsh Language Regulations**”).

These amendments are necessary in consequence of certain provisions of the [Data (Use and Access) Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents), namely sections 61 to 65 and Schedule 3. Those provisions make amendments to the Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953 (c. 20) (“**the 1953 Act**”) to —

- (a) give the Registrar General power to decide the form in which birth, stillbirth and death registers are kept. This means registers no longer have to be paper books. They can be electronic systems;
- (b) ensure local authorities employing registrars and superintendent registrars must provide and maintain whatever equipment or facilities the Registrar General reasonably considers necessary;
- (c) allow for regulations to provide for alternative arrangements to signing the register;
- (d) allow for digital copies of registers to become the official register once the new electronic system comes into force; and
- (e) tidy up relevant legislation which contains terms and phrases which no longer make sense under the new electronic system.

The Registration of Births and Deaths (England and Wales) (Specified Requirements) Regulations 2026 ([S.I. 2026/1168](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/1168)), which is made on 4th November 2026, specify requirements which replace the requirement, under certain provisions of the 1953 Act, for an informant to sign the registers of births and deaths. For the purposes of those provisions, an informant will be deemed to have signed the register if they formally declare that the information provided is true to the best of their knowledge and belief. In relation to births, the informant will also be required to prove their identity.

These Regulations amend the 1987 Regulations to ensure they account for the above changes in legislation. There are also some minor corrections and updates which are unrelated to the changes flowing from the [Data (Use and Access) Act 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents).

Regulation 3 amends regulation 2 of the 1987 Regulations, “Interpretation”, to adopt new definitions introduced into the 1953 Act by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. With the move to the electronic register, no single body will have custody of a physical register, making the concept redundant. The definitions seek to clarify this position by reference to the 1953 Act.

Regulation 4 and regulation 5 make two new forms available electronically. These forms are for informants to provide particulars of a birth or death to a person other than the registrar for the sub-district in which the event happened.

Regulation 6 amends regulation 4 in [the 1987 Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2088/contents), which deals with absence of particulars in a paper form. The informant will now be required to verify the particulars, as they are with other provisions in the 1987 Regulations, rather than “certify” them.

Regulation 18(3) provides for a minor correction due to an erroneous reference to a repealed subsection.

Regulation 26 amends regulation 29 in the 1987 Regulations. Spaces 1 and 2 of Form 8 are required under section 3A(2)(a) and (b) of the 1953 Act; this amendment is to ensure that regulation 29 does not duplicate those requirements by requiring them under the additional particulars that can be prescribed under section 3A(2)(c).

Regulation 39 revokes regulation 54 in [the 1987 Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2088/contents), which deals with corrections of entries before they are complete. Under the electronic system, corrections of this type will not be visible in the final entry since typing can be easily edited prior to completing the entry.

Regulation 50 and regulation 52 revoke regulations 70 and 71 respectively of [the 1987 Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2088/contents). These regulations deal with the process of sending draft particulars and quarterly returns which will no longer be necessary under the new system.

Regulation 40(4)(c) provides for an amendment to regulation 55 of the 1987 Regulations in order to allow for the correcting of an error in copying from a coroner’s certificate requested when an inquest has been discontinued, as provided for by section 23(4) of the Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953.

The remaining regulations between regulation 6 and regulation 53 make amendments to various of [the 1987 Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2088/contents) to account for the changes described in (a) and (c) to (e) above and for the new signature arrangements contained within the Registration of Births and Deaths (England and Wales) (Specified Requirements) Regulations 2026. The specific changes include —

- (a) entering a name and surname in the signature field once particulars are verified, instead of requiring a signature;
- (b) removing most references to “custody of the register” and instead using the definitions inserted in regulation 3 to identify the correct role for certain obligations.
- (c) amendments to the regulations regarding corrections to the register which are necessary due to corrections being made electronically rather than on paper in most cases;
- (d) removing the need for certain notifications and sending of documents where no longer necessary due to the electronic system.

Regulation 54 to regulation 64 make the necessary changes to [the Welsh Language Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2089/contents).

An impact assessment has not been prepared for this instrument as a full impact assessment was published in relation to the Data (Use and Access) Act 2026. Copies can be obtained from the UK Government website at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-use-and-access-bill-supporting-documents or from the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology at 22-26 Whitehall, London, SW1A 2EG, United Kingdom.
