# The Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025 (Commencement No. 3) Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2026 No. 163 (C. 13)

IMMIGRATION AND ASYLUM

The Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025 (Commencement No. 3) Regulations 2026

Made

25th February 2026

The Secretary of State makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by section 65(1) of the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025[^f00001].

## 1. Citation

These Regulations may be cited as the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025 (Commencement No. 3) Regulations 2026.

## 2. Commencement

Section 44 of the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025 (detention and exercise of functions pending deportation), so far as not already in force, comes into force on 5th March 2026.

Alex Norris

Minister of State

Home Office

25th February 2026

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations bring into force specified provisions of the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act [2025 (c. 31)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/31) (“**the Act**”) on 5th March 2026. They are the third commencement regulations made under the Act. Certain provisions were brought into force automatically on Royal Assent by virtue of section 65(3). Other provisions were brought into force automatically on 2nd February 2026 (two months after Royal Assent) by virtue of section 65(4).

Section 44(1) to (13) and (17) of the Act was commenced by section 65(3). Those provisions permit the Secretary of State to detain a person (“**P**”) when they are considering whether to make a deportation order in respect of P, or when the Secretary of State has decided to make a deportation order pending the making of that order.

Section 44(14) to (16), the remaining subsections in section 44, amend section 51 of the Immigration Act [2016 (c. 19)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2016/19) (search for nationality documents by detainee custody officers etc.) so that detainee custody officers can require P to hand over relevant nationality documents, or so that P can be searched for such documents (which may then be seized and retained).

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no, or no significant, impact on the private, voluntary or public sector is foreseen. An impact assessment was published in relation to the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Bill and can be found at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/border-security-asylum-and-immigration-bill-2025-impact-assessment or a copy can be obtained from the Home Office, 2 Marsham Street, London SW1P 4DF.
