# The Meters (Certification) (Amendment) Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2026 No. 162

ELECTRICITY

The Meters (Certification) (Amendment) Regulations 2026

Made

23rd February 2026

Laid before Parliament

25th February 2026

Coming into force

23rd March 2026

The Secretary of State[^f00001] makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 31 and 60 of, and paragraph 5 of Schedule 7 to, the Electricity Act 1989[^f00002].

## 1. Citation, commencement and extent

(1) These Regulations may be cited as the Meters (Certification) (Amendment) Regulations 2026.

(2) These Regulations come into force on 23rd March 2026.

(3) These Regulations extend to England and Wales and Scotland.

## 2. Amendment of the Meters (Certification) Regulations 1998

The Meters (Certification) Regulations 1998[^f00003] are amended in accordance with regulations 3 to 8.

## 3. Amendment to regulation 2

In regulation 2(1), omit the definition of “certification period”.

## 4. Amendment to regulations 4 and 7

In regulations 4(7)(c) and 7(1)(a), for “the certification period”, substitute “10 years beginning on the date on which the meter is first certified for the purpose of the Act”.

## 5. Insertion of regulation 9A

After regulation 9, insert—

> **9A. Certification by type**
>
> A meter is certified for the purpose of the Act where—
>
> - (a) either—
>   - (i) a certificate in respect of that meter was issued by a meter examiner or authorised examiner in accordance with regulation 7 or regulation 9; or
>   - (ii) the meter was certified in accordance with the Act immediately before the coming into force of these Regulations; and
> - (b) the meter is of a type described in columns 1 to 6 of the Table in Schedule 4A.

.

## 6. Amendment to regulation 10

In regulation 10—

- (a) after paragraph (4), insert—

  > or
  >
  > (4A) subject to any extension to the duration of certification by type under regulation 10A, after the number of years specified for that type of meter in column 7 of the Table in Schedule 4A have passed since the meter was first certified for the purpose of the Act.

  ;
- (b) omit paragraphs (5) and (6).

## 7. Insertion of regulation 10A

After regulation 10, insert—

> **10A. Extensions to the duration of certification**
>
> (1) A meter examiner must extend the duration of certification of a type of meter by 5 years if the following conditions are met—
>
> - (a) the meter is of a type described in columns 1 to 6 of the Table in Schedule 4A;
> - (b) an authorised supplier[^f00004] (“**the applicant**”) has—
>   - (i) applied to the meter examiner to extend the duration of certification of that type of meter; and
>   - (ii) submitted meters of that type to a nominated laboratory for examination and testing;
> - (c) the meter examiner is satisfied that—
>   - (i) the type of meter is suitable for examination and testing to determine whether the duration of its certification can be extended;
>   - (ii) each meter submitted for examination and testing is of an approved pattern or construction;
>   - (iii) the number of meters examined and tested by a nominated laboratory is sufficient to provide a reasonable test of all meters of that type; and
>   - (iv) the examination and testing by a nominated laboratory shows meters of that type can reasonably be expected to operate within the permitted margins of error for a further 5 years, beginning in respect of each meter of that type on the day that the meter would otherwise cease to be certified under regulation 10(4A).
>
> (2) If the conditions in sub-paragraphs (1)(a) to (c) are met, the meter examiner must notify the applicant in writing that the duration of certification of meters for that type has been extended by 5 years.
>
> (3) A 5 year extension to the duration of certification under this regulation begins, in respect of each meter of that type, on the day that the meter would otherwise cease to be certified under regulation 10(4A).
>
> (4) A type of meter may have the duration of its certification extended under this regulation more than once.

.

## 8. Omission of Schedule 4 and insertion of Schedule 4A

(1) After Schedule 4, insert Schedule 4A, which is set out in the Schedule to these Regulations.

(2) Omit Schedule 4.

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the Meters (Certification) Regulations 1998 ([S.I. 1998/1566](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/1566)) (“**the 1998 Regulations**”) to provide for certification of certain meters by type for the purposes of the Electricity Act [1989 (c. 29)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1989/29) by inserting regulation 9A into the 1998 Regulations. The meters that are certified by type are those set out in Schedule 4A, which is inserted into the 1998 Regulations by regulation 8.

Regulation 6 amends regulation 10 of the 1998 Regulations to provide when certification is terminated for meters certified by type.

Regulation 7 inserts regulation 10A into the 1998 Regulations to provide a mechanism for meter examiners to extend the duration of certification of a type of meter by 5 years, if the type of meter is listed in Schedule 4A. Following an application by an authorised supplier, the duration of certification of a type of meter must be extended by a meter examiner if the conditions in sub-paragraphs (a) to (c) of regulation 10A are met. This occurs if the meter examiner is satisfied that the examination and testing of samples by a nominated laboratory shows, to the meter examiner’s satisfaction, that meters of that type can reasonably be expected to operate accurately for a further 5 years. Regulation 10A(4) provides that a type of meter may have the duration of certification extended more than once, provided the conditions in regulation 10A is are met each time.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no, or no significant, impact on the private, voluntary or business sectors is foreseen. An Explanatory Memorandum is published alongside these Regulations on www.legislation.gov.uk.
