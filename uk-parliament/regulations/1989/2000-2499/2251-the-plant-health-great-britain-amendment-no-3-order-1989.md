# The Plant Health (Great Britain) (Amendment) (No. 3) Order 1989

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

1989 No. 2251

PLANT HEALTH

The Plant Health (Great Britain) (Amendment) (No. 3) Order 1989

Made

30th November 1989

Laid before Parliament

5th December 1989

Coming into force

6th December 1989

The Minister of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food in relation to England, the Secretary of State for Scotland in relation to Scotland and the Secretary of State for Wales in relation to Wales, in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 1(2)(b) and 2 of the Plant Health Act 1967[^f00001] and now vested in them[^f00002] and of all other powers enabling them in that behalf, hereby make the following Order:

## 1. Title, extent and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Plant Health (Great Britain) (Amendment) (No. 3) Order 1989, shall apply to Great Britain and shall come into force on 6th December 1989.

## 2. Amendment of the Plant Health (Great Britain) Order 1987

The Plant Health (Great Britain) Order 1987[^f00003] shall be amended as follows:–

This Order requires that raw horseradish and radish imported from the Netherlands shall be free of foliage (article 2(1)).

The Order adds*Chrysanthemum* L. to the list of cut flowers which may only be imported when accompanied by a phytosanitary certificate (Article 2(2)(b)).

The Order requires that cut flowers of the genera*Achillea, Antirrhinum, Anemone, Aster, Calendula, Callistephus, Carthamus, Cirsium, Centaurea, Liatris, Lisianthius, Solidago, Solidaster* and*Zinnia* may only be imported from the Netherlands when accompanied by a phytosanitary certificate (article 2(2)(c)).
