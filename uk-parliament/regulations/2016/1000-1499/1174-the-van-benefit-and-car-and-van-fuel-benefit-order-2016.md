# The Van Benefit and Car and Van Fuel Benefit Order 2016

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2016 No. 1174

Income Tax

The Van Benefit and Car and Van Fuel Benefit Order 2016

Made

5th December 2016

Laid before the House of Commons

6th December 2016

Coming into force

31st December 2016

The Treasury, in exercise of the powers conferred by section 170(1A), (2), (5) and (6) of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003[^f00001], make the following Order:

## 1. Citation, commencement and application

(1) This Order may be cited as the Van Benefit and Car and Van Fuel Benefit Order 2016.

(2) This Order comes into force on 31st December 2016 and applies to the tax year 2017-18 and subsequent tax years.

## 2. Amendment to section 150 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003

In section 150(1) of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (car fuel: calculating the cash equivalent)[^f00002] for “£22,200” substitute “£22,600”.

## 3. Amendment to section 155 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003

In section 155(1B)(a) and (b) of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (cash equivalent of the benefit of a van)[^f00003] for “£3,170” substitute “£3,230”.

## 4. Amendment to section 161 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003

In section 161(b) of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (van fuel: the cash equivalent)[^f00004] for “£598” substitute “£610”.

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order amends sections 150(1), 155(1B) and 161(b) of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act [2003 (c. 1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/1) (“the Act”).

Where an employee receives fuel for a car or van as a result of their employment and they are chargeable to tax in respect of that vehicle under sections 120 or 154 of the Act, the cash equivalent of the benefit of that fuel is treated as earnings under sections 149 and 160 of the Act.

The cash equivalent of the benefit of fuel for a car is normally calculated by applying the “appropriate percentage” (normally calculated by reference to the CO<sub>2</sub> emissions of the car) to the figure in section 150(1) of the Act. Article 2 of this Order increases this figure to £22,600 for the tax year 2017-18 and subsequent tax years. The cash equivalent of the benefit of fuel for a van is set out in section 161(b) of the Act. Article 4 of this Order increases this figure to £610 for the tax year 2017-18 and subsequent tax years.

Where a van is made available to an employee for private use that is more than insignificant and not limited to ordinary commuting, section 154 of the Act treats the cash equivalent of the benefit of the van as earnings. For vans that cannot in any circumstances emit CO<sub>2 </sub>by being driven, the cash equivalent for the tax years 2015-16 to 2021-22 is normally calculated by applying the “appropriate percentage” (calculated by reference to the tax year) to the figure in section 155(1B)(a) of the Act. For other vans, the cash equivalent is set out in section 155(1B)(b) of the Act. Article 3 of this Order increases these figures to £3,230 for the tax year 2017-18 and subsequent tax years.

A Tax Information and Impact Note covering this instrument will be published on the government website at https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/tax-information-and-impact-notes-tiins.
