# The Competition Act 1998 (Public Policy Exclusion) Order 2006

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2006 No. 605

COMPETITION

The Competition Act 1998 (Public Policy Exclusion) Order 2006

Made

6th March 2006

Laid before Parliament

9th March 2006

Coming into force

3rd April 2006

The Secretary of State is satisfied that there are exceptional and compelling reasons of public policy why agreements of the description specified in this Order should be excluded from the prohibition contained in Chapter I of the Competition Act 1998[^f00001]. In exercise of the powers conferred by paragraphs 7(1) and (2) of Schedule 3 to the Competition Act 1998, the Secretary of State makes the following Order:

## 1. Citation and commencement

This Order may be cited as the Competition Act 1998 (Public Policy Exclusion) Order 2006 and shall come into force on 3rd April 2006.

## 2. Interpretation

In this Order “surface warship” means aircraft carrier, amphibious ship, destroyer, frigate or mine warfare vessel of the Royal Navy.

## 3. Exclusion from the Chapter I Prohibition

An agreement relating to the maintenance and repair of surface warships is excluded from the Chapter I Prohibition where—

- (a) its purpose is to enable the parties to the agreement to provide or receive maintenance and repair services for surface warships; and
- (b) it does not have as its object or effect the prevention, restriction or distortion of competition within the United Kingdom except in relation to the market for maintenance and repair of surface warships.

Gerry Sutcliffe

Parliamentary Under Secretary of State for Employment Relations and Consumer Affairs

Department of Trade and Industry

6th March 2006

(This note is not part of the Order)

Agreements between undertakings, decisions by associations of undertakings or concerted practices which may affect trade within the United Kingdom, and have as their object or effect the prevention, restriction or distortion of competition within the United Kingdom (“agreements”) are prohibited by Chapter I of the Competition Act 1998. Where there are exceptional and compelling reasons of public policy to do so, the Secretary of State may exclude agreements or descriptions of agreements from that prohibition.

This Order excludes any agreement where the purpose of the agreement is to enable the parties to provide or receive maintenance and repair services for surface warships and the agreement does not have as its object or effect the prevention, restriction or distortion of competition within the United Kingdom except in relation to the market for maintenance and repair of surface warships.
