# The Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (Suspension of Custody Officer Certificate) (Amendment) Regulations 2006

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

This Statutory Instrument has been made in consequence of a defect in [S.I. 1998/474](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/474) and is being issued free of charge to all known recipients of that Statutory Instrument

Laid before Parliament

Coming into force

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred upon him by paragraph 3 of Schedule 2 to the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994[^f00001] makes the following Regulations:

## 1. Citation and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (Suspension of Custody Officer Certificate) (Amendment) Regulations 2006 and shall come into force on 2nd May 2006.

## 2. Amendment of prescribed circumstances

In regulation (2)(a)(i) of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (Suspension of Custody Officer Certificate) Regulations 1998[^f00002] after “an allegation” insert the words “of misconduct”.

Paul Goggins

Parliamentary Under Secretary of State

Home Office

3rd April 2006

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend regulation 2(a)(i) of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (Suspension of Custody Officer Certificate) Regulations 1998. These Regulations amend the circumstances in which the escort monitor or person in charge of a secure training centre may suspend a custody officer’s certificate insofar as it authorises the performance of escort functions or custodial duties pending a decision of the Secretary of State under paragraph 4 of Schedule 2 to the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (revocation of a custody officer certificate).

The amendment is needed to clarify the type of allegation that must be made before a custody officer’s certificate may be withdrawn. The allegation must be one of misconduct. The amendment was recommended by the Joint Committee on Statutory Instruments in its 29<sup>th</sup> Report in 1998.
