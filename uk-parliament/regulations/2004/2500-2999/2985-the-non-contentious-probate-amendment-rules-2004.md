# The Non-Contentious Probate (Amendment) Rules 2004

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2004 No.2985 (L.17)

SUPREME COURT OF ENGLAND AND WALES

The Non-Contentious Probate (Amendment) Rules 2004

Made

12th November 2004

Laid before Parliament

16th November 2004

Coming into force

7th December 2004

The President of the Family Division, in exercise of the powers conferred upon her by section 127(1) of the Supreme Court Act 1981[^f00001], and with the concurrence of the Lord Chancellor, makes the following Rules:

**1.** These Rules may be cited as the Non-Contentious Probate (Amendment) Rules 2004 and shall come into force on 7th December 2004.

**2.** In the definition of “probate practitioner” in rule 2 of the Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1987[^f00002] after “section 23(2) of that Act” insert “or section 55 of the Courts and Legal Services Act 1990[^f00003];”.

Elizabeth Butler-Sloss, P.

Dated 10th November 2004

I concur, on behalf of the Lord Chancellor,

David Lammy

Parliamentary Under Secretary of State

Department for Constitutional Affairs

Dated 13th November 2004

(This note is not part of the Order)

These Rules, with effect from 7th December 2004, amend the definition of “probate practitioner” in rule 2 of the Non-Contentious Probate Rules 1987 ([S.I. 1987/2024](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/2024)) so that a probate practitioner means a person to whom section 23(1) of the Solicitors Act [1974 (c. 47)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1974/47) does not apply by virtue of either section 23(2) of that Act or section 55 of the Courts and Legal Services Act [1990 (c. 41)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/41). Section 55 of the Courts and Legal Services Act 1990 provides that persons may be granted exemption from the provisions of section 23(1) of the Solicitors Act 1974 by an approved body and gives the circumstances in which such exemption will be granted.
