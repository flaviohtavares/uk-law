# The Export Control (Amendment) Order 2009

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2009 No. 1305

Customs

The Export Control (Amendment) Order 2009

Made

22nd May 2009

Laid before Parliament

26th May 2009

Coming into force

16th June 2009

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7 of the Export Control Act 2002[^f00001], makes the following Order:

## 1. Citation, commencement and interpretation

(1) This Order may be cited as the Export Control (Amendment) Order 2009 and shall come into force on 16th June 2009.

(2) In this Order, “the 2008 Order” means the Export Control Order 2008[^f00002].

## 2. Amendment of parenthetical explanation

In article 35(2)(b) of the 2008 Order, after “end-use for” insert “certain military or”.

## 3. Sights for small arms and light weapons

(1) Schedule 1 to the 2008 Order is amended as follows.

(2) For paragraph 11 substitute—

> **11.** Goods specified in entry ML1.a., ML1.b., ML1.c. or ML2.a. in Schedule 2 that are designed to be carried, operated and fired by an individual or by three or fewer individuals acting together, other than mortars with a calibre of 100mm or more.

(3) In paragraph 12—

- (a) after “ML1.d.” insert “or ML2.c. in Schedule 2”;
- (b) at the end of sub-paragraph (a), omit “and”;
- (c) after sub-paragraph (a) insert—

  > - (aa) weapon sights specified in entry ML5.a. in Schedule 2 that are designed for use with weapons falling within paragraph 11; and

  .

## 4. Military vessels, etc.

For entry ML9 in Schedule 2 to the 2008 Order, substitute the text set out in the Schedule to this Order.

## 5. Countries and destinations subject to transit control for category B goods

In Part 4 of Schedule 4 to the 2008 Order—

- (a) after “Albania” insert “Angola”;
- (b) after “Morocco” insert “Namibia”.

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order amends the Export Control Order 2008.

Article 2 corrects a misleading parenthetical explanation of a provision of Council Regulation [(EC) No 1334/2000](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2000/1334). Article 3 makes sure that sights for small arms and light weapons, other than those which only fit larger light weapons within entry ML5 on the military list, are controlled in the same way as the weapons themselves. Article 4 makes a range of vessels not designed or modified for military use but having certain characteristics subject to control in the same way as military vessels. Article 5 adds Angola and Namibia to the list of destinations where the exemptions for transit are of limited application, so that, for example, small arms would need a licence to transit through the UK to either country.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as minimal or no impact on the private or voluntary sectors is foreseen.
