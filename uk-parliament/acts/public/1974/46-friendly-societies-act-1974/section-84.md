# Friendly Societies Act 1974, section 84

> Text as at 2016-10-31.
>
> Extent: England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland.

## 84. Conversion of registered societies into companies.[^c1073404]

*Extent: E+W+S+N.I.*

(1) A registered society may by special resolution determine to convert itself into a company under the Companies Act 2006[^c-485632ec1c565affb7b14e4274bcd9d2].

(2) If a special resolution for converting a registered society into a company contains the particulars required by subsection (1) of section 8 of the Companies Act 2006[^c-f87993f972fcd26de77d94e4d07bf529] to be contained in the memorandum of association of a company and a copy thereof has been registered by the[^c1073405] FCA[^c-4eedd4d1d2e039496905c56c42d5f4a6], a copy of that resolution under the seal and stamp of the FCA shall have the same effect as a memorandum of association duly authenticated as required by subsection (2) of that section[^c-925fa9777501c388b56243969aa134be].

(3) Subject to subsection (4) below, if a registered society is registered as a company under the Companies Act 2006[^c-21afd36a633ebacca45e34ff89491ec3], the registration of that society under this Act shall thereupon become void and shall be cancelled by the FCA.

(4) Registration of a registered society as a company shall not affect any right or claim subsisting against the society or any penalty incurred by the society; and—

- (a) for the purpose of enforcing any such right, claim or penalty, the society may be sued and proceeded against in the same manner as if it had not become registered as a company; and
- (b) every such right or claim, or the liability to any such penalty, shall have priority as against the property of the company over all other rights or claims against or liabilities of the company.
