## 19. Enforcement.[^c4940711]

*Extent: E+W*

(1) If a constable suspects with reasonable cause that any person is committing or has committed an offence under this Part, the constable may without warrant—

- (a) stop and search that person if the constable suspects with reasonable cause that evidence of the commission of the offence is to be found on that person;
- (b) search or examine any thing which that person may then be using or have in his possession if the constable suspects with reasonable cause that evidence of the commission of the offence is to be found on that thing;
- (c) [^c4940721]. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . .
- (d) seize and detain for the purposes of proceedings under this Part any thing which may be evidence of the commission of the offence or may be liable to be forfeited under section 21.

(2) If a constable suspects with reasonable cause that any person is committing or has committed[^c19474351] an offence under this Part, he may, for the purpose of exercising the powers conferred by subsection (1) or arresting a person, in accordance with[^c4940731] section 24[^c19468041] of Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984, for such an offence, enter any premises other than a dwelling[^c19474391].

(2A)[^c19474411] A constable may, for the purpose of assisting him in exercising the powers conferred by subsection (1)(b) and (d) when he has entered any premises under subsection (2), take with him—

- (a) any other person, and
- (b) any equipment or materials.

(3)[^c19474821] If a justice of the peace is satisfied by information on oath that there are reasonable grounds for suspecting that an offence under this Part has been committed[^c4940741] and that evidence of the offence may be found on any premises, he may grant a warrant to any constable [^c19474461]. . . to enter upon and search those premises for the purpose of obtaining that evidence.

In the application of this subsection to Scotland, the reference to a justice of the peace includes a reference to the sheriff.

(9)[^key-0a0231d7c50d8d2b26024c40344bd77a] This section does not apply in relation to offences under Schedule 9A.

## 19. Enforcement.[^c4940751]

*Extent: S*

(1) If a constable suspects with reasonable cause that any person is committing or has committed an offence under this Part, the constable may without warrant—

- (a) stop and search that person if the constable suspects with reasonable cause that evidence of the commission of the offence is to be found on that person;
- (b) search for,[^c19418321] search or examine any thing which that person may then be using or may have used, or may have or have had in his possession,[^c19418371] if the constable suspects with reasonable cause that evidence of the commission of the offence is to be found in or[^c19418391] on that thing;
- (c) arrest that person [^c19399021]. . . ;
- (d) seize and detain for the purposes of proceedings under this Part any thing which may be evidence of the commission of the offence or may be liable to be forfeited under section 21.

(2) If a constable suspects with reasonable cause that any person is committing or has committed[^c19418411] an offence under this Part, he may, for the purpose of exercising the powers conferred by subsection (1), enter any land other than a dwelling or lockfast premises[^c19418451].

(3) If a justice of the peace is satisfied by evidence[^c19418471] on oath that there are reasonable grounds for suspecting that an offence under this Part[^c19399041] has been committed and that evidence of the offence may be found on any premises, he may grant a warrant to any constable to enter those premises, if necessary using reasonable force, and search them[^c19418491] for the purpose of obtaining that evidence.

In the application of this subsection to Scotland, the reference to a justice of the peace includes a reference to the sheriff.

(4)[^c19418511] A warrant under subsection (3) continues in force until the purpose for which the entry is required has been satisfied or, if earlier, the expiry of such period as the warrant may specify.

(5) A constable authorised by virtue of this section to enter any land must, if required to do so by the occupier or anyone acting on the occupier’s behalf, produce evidence of the constable’s authority.

(6) A constable who enters any land in the exercise of a power conferred by this section—

- (a) may—
  - (i) be accompanied by any other persons, and
  - (ii) take any machinery, other equipment or materials on to the land,

  for the purpose of assisting the constable in the exercise of that power,
- (b) may take samples of any articles or substances found there and remove the samples from the land.

(7) A power specified in subsection (6)(a) or (b) which is exercisable under a warrant is subject to the terms of the warrant.

(8) A constable leaving any land which has been entered in exercise of a power conferred by subsection (2) or by a warrant under subsection (3), being either unoccupied land or land from which the occupier is temporarily absent, must leave it as effectively secured against unauthorised entry as the constable found it.
