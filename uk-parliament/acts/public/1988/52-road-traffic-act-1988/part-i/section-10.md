# Road Traffic Act 1988, section 10

## 10. Detention of persons affected by alcohol or a drug.[^c19118131][^key-6aece8b57d0439e5eb10006721787654]

*Extent: E+W+S+N.I.*

(1) Subject to subsections (2) and (3) below, a person required under section 7 or 7A[^c19121681] to provide a specimen of breath, blood or urine may afterwards be detained at a police station (or, if the specimen was provided otherwise than at a police station, arrested and taken to and detained at a police station) if a constable has reasonable grounds for believing[^c19221101] that, were that person then driving or attempting to drive a mechanically propelled vehicle[^c13971261] on a road, he would commit[^c19221111] an offence under section 4, 5 or 5A[^key-70711093361dbf73464a53266291ad34] of this Act.

(2) Subsection (1) above does not apply to the person if it ought reasonably to appear to the[^c19221141] constable that there is no likelihood of his driving or attempting to drive a mechanically propelled vehicle whilst—[^key-8ac0a0516eda03a379b0054df28cca23]

- (a) the person's ability to drive properly is impaired,
- (b) the proportion of alcohol in the person's breath, blood or urine exceeds the prescribed limit, or
- (c) the proportion of a specified controlled drug in the person's blood or urine exceeds the specified limit for that drug.

(2A)[^c19221181] A person who is at a hospital as a patient shall not be arrested and taken from there to a police station in pursuance of this section if it would be prejudicial to his proper care and treatment as a patient.

(3) A constable must consult a medical practitioner on any question arising under this section whether a person’s ability to drive properly is or might be impaired through drugs and must act on the medical practitioner’s advice.
