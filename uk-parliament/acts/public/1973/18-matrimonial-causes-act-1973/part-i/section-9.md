## 9. Proceedings before divorce order has been made final: general powers of court.[^key-1f801c3712a5dc29ae00f331e33d8341]

*Extent: E+W*

(1) Where a divorce order has been made but not made final[^key-2a45d8acf2eb4de71cfeecb81897bfb5], then, without prejudice to section 8 above, any person (excluding a party to the proceedings other than the Queen’s Proctor) may show cause why the order should not be made final[^key-b63f0c4b06ad52b81da51b9f674602ca] by reason of material facts not having been brought before the court; and in such a case the court may—

- (a) notwithstanding anything in section 1(4)[^key-d01348ea6000262bb6c155e12ef9a331] above (but subject to section[^key-235059b3426abbd5346b1a9a1091f520] 10(2) to (4) [^key-b2c3c67b57aed5fb246e10e444af5543]... below) make the order final; or
- (b) rescind the order[^key-8197b4f23d396649f6062b2c15beb784]; or
- (c) require further inquiry; or
- (d) otherwise deal with the case as it thinks fit.

(2) Where a divorce order has been made on an application by one party to a marriage and that party has not applied for the order to be made final,[^key-eb8322bb7ccf029de0e96c830cdae546] then, at any time after the expiration of three months from the earliest date on which that party could have made such an application, the other party to the marriage[^key-46b9e5b0bd6a6ac7830d028fd7959ac0] may make an application to the court, and on that application the court may exercise any of the powers mentioned in paragraphs (a) to (d) of subsection (1) above.
