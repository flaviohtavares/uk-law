# Arbitration Act 1996, section 93

> Text as at 2025-08-01.
>
> Extent: England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland.

## 93. Appointment of judges as arbitrators.

*Extent: E+W*

(1) An eligible High Court judge[^key-da76e021f8194278dcba717f0a1751aa] or an official referee may, if in all the circumstances he thinks fit, accept appointment as a sole arbitrator or as umpire by or by virtue of an arbitration agreement.

(2) An eligible High Court judge[^key-9fdeb2a626fcb3099623d76cf67ff518] shall not do so unless the Lord Chief Justice has informed him that, having regard to the state of business in the High Court and the Crown Court, he can be made available.

(3) An official referee shall not do so unless the Lord Chief Justice has informed him that, having regard to the state of official referees’ business, he can be made available.

(4) The fees payable for the services of an eligible High Court judge[^key-d5c00662163bd4c3bd37e87736c990b3] or official referee as arbitrator or umpire shall be taken in the High Court.

(4A)[^key-8393428426a76f2a50c6afe19e46edcc] The Lord Chief Justice may nominate a senior judge (as defined in section 109(5) of the Constitutional Reform Act 2005) to exercise functions of the Lord Chief Justice under this section.

(5) In this section—

- “**arbitration agreement**” has the same meaning as in Part I; [^key-ecd496ee032e6d1660c227e79c61aec7]...
- “eligible High Court judge” means—[^key-e5a08d36dfb9e7b0f71c7f019abbb989]
  - (a) a puisne judge of the High Court, or
  - (b) a person acting as a judge of the High Court under or by virtue of section 9(1) of the Senior Courts Act 1981;
- “**official referee**” means a person nominated under section 68(1)(a) of the [^c833553]Senior Courts Act 1981[^c2086522][^c2086522] to deal with official referees’ business.

(6) The provisions of Part I of this Act apply to arbitration before a person appointed under this section with the modifications specified in Schedule 2.
