# Customs and Excise Management Act 1979, section 28

> **Warning: changes not yet applied to this text (11).** Text as at 2026-10-01. See https://www.legislation.gov.uk/changes/affected/ukpga/1979/2

## 28. Officers’ powers of access, etc.[^c14771431][^c-7b16e437748ae16bed689ce8dcfde74b][^c14771421]

*Extent: E+W+S+N.I.*

(1) Without prejudice to section 27 above, the proper officer shall have free access to every part of any ship or aircraft at a port or aerodrome[^c14771441] and of any other vehicle[^c-1e62fe242750450b4e580c756802f0d7] which falls within paragraphs (a) to (f) of subsection (1) of section 27 above or is[^c14771451] brought to a customs and excise station, and may—

- (a) cause any goods to be marked before they are unloaded from that vehicle[^c-df246f645f368b7775849be2d2a6b077];
- (b) lock up, seal, mark or otherwise secure any goods carried in the vehicle[^c-1ebcfcb8b719df57e5ad343a85ecf166] or any place or container in which they are so carried; and
- (c) break open any place or container which is locked and of which the keys are withheld.

(2) Any goods found concealed on board any such vehicle[^c-a22fa69ed3c092477876abd3c4df6b0b] shall be liable to forfeiture.
