# Local Audit and Accountability Act 2014, section 12

> Extent: England, Wales.

## 12. Failure to appoint local auditor[^c-ed8f5b685bc2fc092cd72279577365f0]

*Extent: E+W*

(1) If a relevant authority, other than a health service body[^c-571e3ae06d1c1678e25d9c9bd28ffae7], fails to appoint a local auditor in accordance with this Part, the authority must immediately inform the Secretary of State of that fact.

(2) If it appears to the Secretary of State that a relevant authority, other than a health service body[^c-525253112798e5fba4f071515ea3cb9b], has failed to appoint a local auditor in accordance with this Part, the Secretary of State may—

- (a) direct the authority to appoint the auditor named in the direction, or
- (b) appoint a local auditor on behalf of the authority.

(3)[^c-dc4bebd2a171dfdb6b45103a802eff50] An appointment under subsection (2)(b) takes effect—

- (a) as if it had been made by the relevant authority, and
- (b) on such terms as the Secretary of State may direct.

(4)[^c-dc4bebd2a171dfdb6b45103a802eff50] The Secretary of State must—

- (a) inform the relevant authority of the intention to give a direction or appoint a local auditor under subsection (2) not less than 28 days before the direction is given or the appointment made, and
- (b) consider any representations made by the relevant authority regarding the proposed direction or appointment.

(5)[^c-9c6ba1f10b916e81821dc5d2360615c4] But the Secretary of State may give a direction or make an appointment under subsection (2) without having complied with subsection (4) if the Secretary of State thinks it is likely that a local auditor would have to exercise a function under this Act in relation to a relevant authority within the period of 60 days beginning with the day on which the direction is given or the appointment is made.
