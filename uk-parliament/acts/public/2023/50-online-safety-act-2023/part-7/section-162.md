## 162. OFCOM’s report about researchers’ access to information[^c-c89c1218cc9f93f4563331d38e94c01a]

*Extent: E+W+S+N.I.*

(1)[^c-4460a77def996dd5d136e5b92298fb20] OFCOM must produce a report—

- (a) describing how, and to what extent, persons carrying out independent research into online safety matters are currently able to obtain information from providers of regulated services to inform their research,
- (b) exploring the legal and other issues which currently constrain the sharing of information for such purposes, and
- (c) assessing the extent to which greater access to information for such purposes might be achieved.

(2)[^c-4460a77def996dd5d136e5b92298fb20] For the purposes of this section a person carries out “independent research” if the person carries out research on behalf of a person other than a provider of a regulated service.

(3)[^c-4460a77def996dd5d136e5b92298fb20] In preparing the report, OFCOM must consult—

- (a) the Information Commission[^c-cc6fdb5c813a41107db4a9dc4d055c20],
- (b) the Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation,
- (c) United Kingdom Research and Innovation,
- (d) persons who appear to OFCOM to represent providers of regulated services, and
- (e) such other persons as OFCOM consider appropriate.

(4)[^c-4460a77def996dd5d136e5b92298fb20] OFCOM must publish the report within the period of 18 months beginning with the day on which this section comes into force.

(5)[^c-4460a77def996dd5d136e5b92298fb20] OFCOM must send a copy of the report to the Secretary of State, and the Secretary of State must lay it before Parliament.

(6)[^c-4460a77def996dd5d136e5b92298fb20] For further provision about the report under this section, see section 164.
