# The Magistrates' Courts (Amendment) Rules (Northern Ireland) 2000

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2000 No. 279

MAGISTRATES' COURTS

The Magistrates' Courts (Amendment) Rules (Northern Ireland) 2000

Made

26th September 2000

Coming into operation

2nd October 2000

The Lord Chancellor, in exercise of the powers conferred on him by Article 13 of the Magistrates' Courts (Northern Ireland) Order 1981[^f00001] and all other powers enabling him in that behalf, on the advice of the Magistrates' Courts Rules Committee and after consultation with the Lord Chief Justice, hereby makes the following Rules:

## 1. Citation and commencement

These Rules may be cited as the Magistrates' Courts (Amendment) Rules (Northern Ireland) 2000 and shall come into operation on2nd October 2000.

## 2. Amendment

For Rule 156 of the Magistrates' Courts Rules (Northern Ireland) 1984[^f00002], there shall be substituted the following new Rule—

> **156. Procedure after appeal to county court is determined**
>
> (1) After the county court has decided an appeal from a magistrates' court, the chief clerk for the county court division for which that county court is held, within seven days of such decision,
>
> - (a) shall certify such decision; and
> - (b) in any case in which the county court has determined that a right or rights of the appellant under Article 5 of the Convention were infringed by the making of the order to which the appeal relates shall so specify, at the foot of the form of appeal and shall transmit the form of appeal to the clerk of petty sessions who shall enter the details referred to in sub-paragraphs (*a*) and (*b*) as appropriate in his Order Book.
>
> (2) In this Rule, “the Convention” has the same meaning as in section 21 of the Human Rights Act 1998[^f00003].

.

Irvine of Lairg, *C.*

Dated 26th September 2000.

(This note is not part of the Rules.)

These Rules amend the Magistrates' Courts Rules (Northern Ireland) 1984, in consequence of the Human Rights Act 1998, so as to provide that a finding by a county court on appeal that the making of the order appealed against has infringed the appellant’s rights under Article 5 of the European Convention on Human Rights shall be certified on the notice of appeal and recorded in the Order Book.
