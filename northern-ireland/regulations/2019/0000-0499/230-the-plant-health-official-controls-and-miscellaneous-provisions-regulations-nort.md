Made

[^key-3b0775c651c7cfdda0f9e462a2ed2ad4]The Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs is a Northern Ireland department designated [^c23949901] for the purposes of section 2(2) of the European Communities Act 1972 [^c23949911] in relation to the common agricultural policy of the European Union.

The Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs [^c23949921] makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 2, 3(1), 3A and 3B(1) of the Plant Health Act (Northern Ireland) 1967 [^c23949931] and section 2(2) of, and Paragraph 1A of Schedule 2 to, the European Communities Act 1972 [^c23949941].

These Regulations make provision for a purpose mentioned in section 2(2) of the European Communities Act 1972 and it appears expedient to the Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs for the references to Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/2072 establishing uniform conditions for the implementation of Regulation (EU) 2016/2031 of the European Parliament and of the Council, as regards protective measures against pests of plants and repealing Commission Regulation [(EC) No 690/2008](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2008/0690) and amending Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2018/2019 (referred to in these Regulations as “**the Phytosanitary Conditions Regulation**”) and to the European Union instruments mentioned in regulation 3(1) to be construed as references to those instruments as amended from time to time.

These Regulations give effect to—

- (a) Regulation (EU) 2016/2031 of the European Parliament of the Council on protective measures against pests of plants, amending Regulations (EU) No 228/2013, (EU) No 652/2014 and (EU) No 1143/2014 of the European Parliament and of the Council (“**the EU Plant Health Regulation**”); and
- (b) Regulation (EU) 2017/625 of the European Parliament and of Council on official controls and other official activities performed to ensure the application of food and feed law, rules on animal health and welfare, plant health and plant protection products, in so far as it applies to the rules referred to in Article 1(2)(g) (“**the official Controls Regulation**”).

The Regulations also implement in relation to Northern Ireland—

- (a) Council Directive [69/464/EEC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1969/0464) on control of Potato Wart Disease (O.J. No. L 323, 24.12.1969, p. 1);
- (b) Council Directive [93/85/EEC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1993/0085) on the control of potato ring rot (O.J. No. L 259, 18.10.1993, p. 1);
- (c) Council Directive [98/57/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1998/0057) on the control of Ralstonia solanacearum (Smith) Yabuuchi et al. (O.J. No. L 235, 21.8.1998, p. 1); and
- (d) Council Directive [2007/33/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/2007/0033) on the control of potato cyst nematodes and repealing Directive [69/465/EEC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/directive/1969/0465) (O.J. No. L 156, 16.6.2007, p. 12).

Part 1 is introductory and includes definitions. Regulation 3(2) provides for references to the European Union instruments listed in regulation 3(1) to be read as references to those instruments as amended from time to time.

Regulation 5 designates the Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs as the competent authority in Northern Ireland.

Part 3 makes further provision in relation to consignments of plants, plant products and other objects from third countries which are subject to official controls on their entry into the European Union.

Part 4 contains powers to enable plant health inspectors appointed by the Department to take measures to prevent the establishment or spread of harmful plant pests in Northern Ireland.

Part 5 imposes additional temporary measures to prevent the entry of certain harmful plant pests into Northern Ireland or their establishment in, or spread within, Northern Ireland.

Part 6 makes further provision in relation to the registration of professional operators and the granting of authorisations to professional operators by the Department.

Part 7 imposes additional requirements in relation to certain solanaceous species (potatoes and tomatoes) to implement the Directives mentioned above.

Part 8 imposes additional notification requirements in respect of certain plants and plant products which are to be brought into Northern Ireland from third countries, other member States or Switzerland.

Part 9 sets out general powers of plant health inspectors to enable them to perform official controls and other official activities, and enforce the EU Plant Health Regulation, the Official Controls Regulation and these Regulations. Regulation 32 provides for recovery of expenses incurred by the Department and regulation 35 provides for an appeal process.

Part 10 contains general and supplemental provisions in relation to notices given by plant health inspectors.

Part 11 contains offences for non-compliance with specified provisions of the EU Plant Health Regulation and the Official Controls Regulation, and offences in relation to these Regulations. Regulation 48 sets out the penalties for these offences.

Part 12 deals with minor and consequential amendments to secondary legislation, revocations and transitional provisions in relation to Northern Ireland. Regulation 55 requires the Department to review the operation and effect of the EU Plant Health Regulation, the Official Controls Regulation and these Regulations and publish a report at five-yearly intervals.

An impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no, or no significant, impact on the private or voluntary sector is foreseen.
