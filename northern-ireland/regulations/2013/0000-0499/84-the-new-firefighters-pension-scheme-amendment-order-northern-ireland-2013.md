# The New Firefighters' Pension Scheme (Amendment) Order (Northern Ireland) 2013

> Text as at 2014-04-01.
>
> Extent: Northern Ireland.

*Extent: N.I.*

2013 No. 84

Fire And Rescue Services

Pensions

The New Firefighters' Pension Scheme (Amendment) Order (Northern Ireland) 2013

Made

26th March 2013

Coming into operation

1st April 2013

The Department of Health, Social Services and Public Safety, in exercise of the powers conferred by Article 10(1), (3), (4) and (5) of the Fire Services (Northern Ireland) Order 1984 [^c21725581], and now vested in it [^c21725591] and with the approval of the Department of Finance and Personnel in accordance with Article 10(1) of that Order makes the following Order:

## 1. Citation and commencement

This Order may be cited as the New Firefighters' Pension Scheme (Amendment) Order (Northern Ireland) 2013 and shall come into operation on 1st April 2013.

## 2. Amendment of the New Firefighters' Pension Scheme Order (Northern Ireland) 2007

The Annex to the New Firefighters' Pension Scheme Order (Northern Ireland) 2007 [^c21725601] (in which the New Firefighters' Pension Scheme (Northern Ireland) is set out) is amended in accordance with articles 3 and 4.

## 3.

In Chapter 1 of Part 11 (pensionable pay, pension contributions and purchase of additional service), in article 63(1) (pension contributions) insert “ percentage ” before “rate”.

This Order further amends the New Firefighters' Pension Scheme Order (Northern Ireland) 2007.

Article 3 inserts the word “percentage” to clarify the meaning of a provision amended by the New Firefighters' Pension Scheme (Amendment) Order (Northern Ireland) 2012 (S.I. 2012/162).

Article 4 amends the Scheme to provide a different rate for pension contributions payable by members of the Scheme which increase according to the amount of pensionable pay which the member receives. The contribution rates are specified in the Table.

In addition, a new band of pensionable pay has been created so that those firefighters who earn more than £15,000 and up to and including £21,000 will pay a lower rate than those earning more than £21,000 and up to and including £30,000.
