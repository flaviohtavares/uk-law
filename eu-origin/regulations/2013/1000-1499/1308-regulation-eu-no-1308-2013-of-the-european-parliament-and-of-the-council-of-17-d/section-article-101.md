# Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 December 2013 establishing a common organisation of the markets in agricultural products and repealing Council Regulations (EEC) No 922/72, (EEC) No 234/79, (EC) No 1037/2001 and (EC) No 1234/2007, section Article 101

## Article 101. Additional grounds for refusal of protection

*Extent: E+W+S+N.I.*

1. A name that has become generic in the United Kingdom[^c-531b319d61d5d45ff5896d999f185101] shall not be protected in Great Britain[^c-e87c8ae101f2c94138a2f6bfa2b58289] as a designation of origin or a geographical indication.

For the purposes of this Section, a "name that has become generic" means the name of a wine which, although it relates to the place or the region where this product was originally produced or marketed, has become the common name of a wine [^c-f15fd71cd61c5d812a1c88787e9dfdad]... in the United Kingdom[^c-0de3df1cc3f1c82df7fd5c47a33a28ab].

To establish whether or not a name has become generic, the relevant factors shall be taken into account, in particular:

- (a) the existing situation [^c-263e6521473bd24a330f2b2d2ba54102]..., notably in areas of consumption;
- (b) the relevant [^c-5545f40b0c48b108ede9f4b8661f01fb]... law.

2. A name shall not be protected as a designation of origin or geographical indication where, in the light of a trade mark's reputation and renown, protection could mislead the consumer as to the true identity of the wine.
