# The Local Authorities (Annual Sufficiency Plans) (Wales) Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2026 No. 116

SOCIAL CARE, WALES

CHILDREN AND YOUNG PERSONS, WALES

The Local Authorities (Annual Sufficiency Plans) (Wales) Regulations 2026

Made

14 September 2026

Coming into force

26 October 2026

The Welsh Ministers make the following Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 75A(2)(a) and (3)(f), and 196(2) of the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014[^f00001].

## 1. Title and coming into force

(1) The title of these Regulations is the Local Authorities (Annual Sufficiency Plans) (Wales) Regulations 2026.

(2) These Regulations come into force on 26 October 2026.

## 2. Interpretation

(1) In these Regulations—

- “**the 2014 Act**” (“**Deddf 2014**”) means the [Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/contents);
- “**the 2016 Act**” (“**Deddf 2016**”) means the [Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2016/2/contents)[^f00002];
- “**annual sufficiency plan**” (“**cynllun digonolrwydd blynyddol**”) means a plan that a local authority is required by [section 75A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) [(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) of [the 2014 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/contents) to prepare and publish before the beginning of each financial year setting out the steps it will take in that year in pursuance of its duty under section 75(1) of the 2014 Act;
- “**children’s home service**” (“**gwasanaeth cartref plant**”) has the meaning given in section 2A(2) of the 2016 Act[^f00003];
- “**deprivation of liberty order**” (“**gorchymyn amddifadu o ryddid**”) means an order of a court, including an interim order, authorising the deprivation of liberty of a looked after child in connection with arrangements made for the child’s care, protection or welfare;
- “**fostering service**” (“**gwasanaeth maethu**”) has the meaning given in paragraph 5 of Schedule 1 to the 2016 Act;
- “**local authority**” (“**awdurdod lleol**”) means the council of a county or county borough in Wales;
- “**local authority foster parent**” (“**rhiant maeth awdurdod lleol**”) has the meaning given in section 197(1) of the 2014 Act[^f00004];
- “**looked after child**” (“**plentyn sy’n derbyn gofal**”) means a child who is looked after by a local authority within the meaning of [section 74](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/74) of [the 2014 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/contents), and a reference in these Regulations to a child being looked after by a local authority is a reference to a looked after child who is being looked after by that authority;
- “**the plan year**” (“**blwyddyn y cynllun**”), in relation to the annual sufficiency plan, means the financial year to which the annual sufficiency plan relates;
- “**provider type**” (“**math o ddarparwr**”) means whether the provider of accommodation is—
  - (a) a local authority;
  - (b) a person who is a not-for-profit entity within the meaning of section 6A(2) of the 2016 Act[^f00005];
  - (c) a person who is neither a local authority nor a not-for-profit entity within the meaning of section 6A(2) of the 2016 Act;
- “**relevant preceding year**” (“**blwyddyn flaenorol berthnasol**”), in relation to an annual sufficiency plan, means the financial year immediately preceding the plan year;
- “**second preceding year**”(“**ail flwyddyn flaenorol**”). in relation to an annual sufficiency plan, means the financial year immediately preceding the relevant preceding year;
- “**secure accommodation service**” (“**gwasanaeth llety diogel**”) means a secure accommodation service within the meaning of paragraph 2 of Schedule 1 to the 2016 Act;
- “**service operating without registration**” (“**gwasanaeth sy’n gweithredu heb gofrestru**”) means a place at which a children’s home service or secure accommodation service is being provided, and where the service provider is not registered to provide a children’s home service or secure accommodation service at that place.

(2) In these Regulations, references to a child being placed out of county are references to a child being placed outside the area of the local authority that looks after the child.

(3) In these Regulations, references to a child being placed out of country are references to a child being placed outside of Wales.

## 3. Form of annual sufficiency plan

(1) For the purposes of [section 75A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) [(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) [(a)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) of [the 2014 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/contents), an annual sufficiency plan must be prepared using the template published by the Welsh Ministers for the purpose of presenting the information required by these Regulations.

(2) The Welsh Ministers must publish the template on the Welsh Government website.

## 4. Information to be included in annual sufficiency plans

In addition to the information required by [section 75A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) [(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) of [the 2014 Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/contents), for the purposes of [section 75A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) [(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A) [(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/anaw/2014/4/section/75A)[^f00006] of the 2014 Act, an annual sufficiency plan must include the information specified in the Schedule.

## 5. Use of information in criminal proceedings

Information provided by a local authority in compliance with these Regulations may not be used in evidence against an individual in criminal proceedings.

## Schedule — Additional information to be included in an annual sufficiency plan

Regulation 4

### Part 1 — Information relating to the period before the plan year

#### 1. Needs profile

(1) A written summary of the needs profile of looked after children in the local authority area as at 30 September in the relevant preceding year.

(2) In this paragraph, “**needs profile**”, in relation to looked after children in a local authority area, means an evidence-based summary of the characteristics, circumstances and support requirements of children and young people in that area who need care and support.

(3) The written summary required by sub-paragraph (1) must include an assessment of—

- (a) the demographic and population characteristics of those children, including age, language and protected characteristics;
- (b) the circumstances and factors associated with those children’s vulnerability or why they entered into care;
- (c) the placement and care needs of those children;
- (d) the social, cultural and relational needs of those children;
- (e) the needs of older children and young people approaching adulthood;
- (f) the needs of those children who require integrated care packages with education and health components or other complex care package solutions;
- (g) local factors influencing the presentation of needs and trends in those needs.

(4) In addition to the written summary required by sub-paragraph (1), an assessment of the engagement that the local authority has undertaken with children and young people to ascertain their wishes and feelings for the purposes of determining their needs.

(5) In this paragraph, “**protected characteristics**” has the meaning given in [section 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/4) of the [Equality Act 2010](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/contents)[^f00007].

#### 2. Children looked after: second preceding year

(1) The number of children that the local authority looked after as at 31 March of the second preceding year.

Explanatory Note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations are made under sections 75A(2)(a) and (3)(f), and 196(2) of the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 (“**the 2014 Act**”). They prescribe the form of, and information to be included in, annual sufficiency plans that local authorities in Wales are required to prepare and publish under section 75A of the 2014 Act.

The purpose of annual sufficiency plans is to strengthen strategic planning and transparency in relation to local authorities’ duties under section 75(1) of the 2014 Act. Those duties require local authorities to secure, so far as reasonably practicable, sufficient accommodation provided by local authorities or registered providers that meet the requirements of section 6A(1) of the Regulation and Inspection of Social Care (Wales) Act 2016, within or near to their areas, to meet the needs of looked after children.

Regulation 3 provides that annual sufficiency plans must be prepared using a template specified and published by the Welsh Ministers.

Regulation 4 and the Schedule prescribe the information that must be included in an annual sufficiency plan in addition to the information required by section 75A(3) of the 2014 Act. The Schedule requires local authorities to provide information relating to—

- the profile and assessed needs of looked after children requiring care and support within the local authority area,
- number of looked after children,
- placement patterns, including placements outside the local authority area and outside Wales,
- use of children’s home services, fostering services and secure accommodation services,
- placement of children in places in respect of which the provider is not registered to provide a regulated service,
- number of children subject to deprivation of liberty orders,
- current and forecast sufficiency challenges and risks,
- the availability of accommodation and placement provision at local, regional and national levels,
- workforce position regarding capacity, capability, recruitment, retention and planning,
- data quality and data limitations, and
- alignment of annual sufficiency plans with other relevant strategies and plans.

The Schedule requires local authorities to provide information about the needs of children and young people, recent and projected demand for accommodation for looked after children, placement patterns and provision available to meet those needs. The information required covers the second preceding year, the relevant preceding year and future forecasting. The relevant preceding year is the financial year before the plan year, and the second preceding year is the financial year immediately before the relevant preceding year. The Schedule also requires forecasts for the remainder of the relevant preceding year and for the plan year itself, together with narrative assessments of risks to sufficiency, planned mitigation measures, workforce arrangements, data quality and future demand.

Regulation 5 provides that information supplied by a local authority in compliance with these Regulations may not be used in evidence against an individual in criminal proceedings. Local authorities are required to provide information under these Regulations and, in doing so, individuals may be involved in gathering, collating, verifying or supplying information that relates to matters capable of giving rise to criminal liability. The provision is intended to protect those individuals from self-incrimination.

The Welsh Ministers’ Code of Practice on the carrying out of Regulatory Impact Assessments was considered in relation to these Regulations. As a result, a regulatory impact assessment has been prepared as to the likely costs and benefits of complying with these Regulations. A copy can be obtained from the Welsh Government, Cathays Park, Cardiff, CF10 3NQ and on the website at www.gov.wales.
