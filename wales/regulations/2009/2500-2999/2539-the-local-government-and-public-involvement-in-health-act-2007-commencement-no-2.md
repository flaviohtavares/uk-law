# The Local Government and Public Involvement in Health Act 2007 (Commencement No. 2) (Wales) Order 2009

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2009 No. 2539 (W.203) (C.105)

LOCAL GOVERNMENT, WALES

The Local Government and Public Involvement in Health Act 2007 (Commencement No. 2) (Wales) Order 2009

Made

15 September 2009

The Welsh Ministers, in exercise of the powers conferred by section 245(3) of the Local Government and Public Involvement in Health Act 2007[^f00001], make the following Order:

## 1. Title and application

(1) The title of this Order is the Local Government and Public Involvement in Health Act 2007 (Commencement No. 2) (Wales) Order 2009.

(2) This Order applies in relation to Wales.

## 2. Commencement

The following provisions of the Local Government and Public Involvement in Health Act 2007, in so far as they relate to Wales, come into force on 1 October 2009—

- (a) section 126 (reference of local crime and disorder matters to crime and disorder committees, etc.);
- (b) the entry in Part 6 of Schedule 18 (repeals) relating to the Police and Justice Act [2006 (c. 48)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/48); and
- (c) section 241(repeals) so far as relating to the entry specified in (b) above.

Brian Gibbons

Minister for Social Justice and Local Government, one of the Welsh Ministers

15 September 2009

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order brings section 126 of the Local Government and Public Involvement in Health Act [2007 (c. 28)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/28) (“the 2007 Act”) into force on 1 October 2009 in relation to Wales.

Section 126 makes amendments to section 19 of the Police and Justice Act 2006 (“the 2006 Act”), including in particular the substitution of new subsections 19(3) to (8B). The new provisions require every local authority to ensure that its crime and disorder committee (as created under section 19 of the 2006 Act) has the power to make a report or recommendations on relevant matters to the local authority and to ensure that any member of the local authority who is not a member of the committee has the power to refer relevant matters to the committee. The new provisions also set out certain procedures that must be followed where a person refers a relevant matter to the local authority.

The Order also commences an associated repeal.
