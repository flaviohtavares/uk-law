# The Infrastructure Act 2015 (Commencement) (Wales) Regulations 2015

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

2015 No. 990 (W. 67) (C. 67)

Wildlife, Wales

The Infrastructure Act 2015 (Commencement) (Wales) Regulations 2015

Made

24 March 2015

The Welsh Ministers, in exercise of the powers conferred by section 57(4)(b) of the Infrastructure Act 2015[^f00001], make the following Regulations.

## 1. Title

The title of these Regulations is the Infrastructure Act 2015 (Commencement) (Wales) Regulations 2015.

## 2. Provision coming into force on 12 April 2015

Part 4 of the Infrastructure Act 2015 (environmental control of animal and plant species) comes into force on 12 April 2015 so far as it relates to Wales.

Carl Sergeant

Minister for Natural Resources, one of the Welsh Ministers

24 March 2015

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations, which apply in relation to Wales, are made under the Infrastructure Act [2015 (c.7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/7) (“the 2015 Act”).

Regulation 2 brings Part 4 of the 2015 Act (environmental control of animal and plant species), namely sections 23, 24 and 25, into force on 12 April 2015 in relation to Wales.

Section 23 of the 2015 Act inserts a new subsection 14(4A) into the Wildlife and Countryside Act [1981 (c.69)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1981/69) (“the 1981 Act”) providing for measures relating to species control agreements and species control orders to be contained in a new Schedule 9A to that Act.

Section 24 of the 2015 Act amends Part I of Schedule 9 to the 1981 Act so that it is divided into three distinct Parts.

Section 25 of the 2015 Act makes consequential changes to the 1981 Act.

The Welsh Ministers’ Code of Practice on the carrying out of Regulatory Impact Assessments was considered in relation to these Regulations. As a result, it was not considered necessary to carry out a regulatory impact assessment as to the likely costs and benefits of complying with these Regulations.
