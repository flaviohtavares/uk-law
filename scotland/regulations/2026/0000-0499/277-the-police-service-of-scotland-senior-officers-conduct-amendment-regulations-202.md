# The Police Service of Scotland (Senior Officers) (Conduct) Amendment Regulations 2026

> This is the text as originally enacted or made. It may have been changed since; check legislation.gov.uk for the current version.

Coming into force

The Scottish Ministers make the following Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 48, 55 and 125(1) of the Police and Fire Reform (Scotland) Act 2012[^f00001] and all other powers enabling them to do so.

In accordance with section 54(2) of that Act, they have consulted and shared a draft of the Regulations with the persons mentioned in section 54(2)(a)(i) to (vi) of that Act and have considered any representations made.

## PART 1 — General

### 1. Citation and commencement

These Regulations may be cited as the Police Service of Scotland (Senior Officers) (Conduct) Amendment Regulations 2026 and come into force on 26 November 2026.

### 2. Interpretation

In these Regulations—

- “**the 2014 Conduct Regulations**” means the Police Service of Scotland (Conduct) Regulations 2014[^f00002],
- “**the Senior Officers’ Conduct Regulations**” means the Police Service of Scotland (Senior Officers) (Conduct) Regulations 2013[^f00003],
- “**the Senior Officers’ Performance Regulations**” means the Police Service of Scotland (Senior Officers) (Performance) Regulations 2016[^f00004].

## PART 2 — Amendments to the Senior Officers’ Conduct Regulations

### 3. Amendments to the Senior Officers’ Conduct Regulations

The Senior Officers’ Conduct Regulations are amended in accordance with regulations 4 to 32.

### 10. Referral of misconduct allegations

After regulation 7 (alleged offences), insert—

> **7A. Referral of misconduct allegations**
>
> Where a misconduct allegation against a senior officer, or a constable to whom these Regulations apply in accordance with regulation 3, is received by, or otherwise comes to the attention of, the Authority or the chief constable, the Authority or the chief constable (as the case may be) must refer the allegation to the Commissioner without delay.
>
> **7B. Assessment by Commissioner**
>
> (1) This regulation applies where a misconduct allegation—
>
> - (a) is referred to the Commissioner under regulation 7A, or
> - (b) is otherwise received by, or comes to the attention of, the Commissioner from any source.
>
> (2) The Commissioner must assess whether the conduct which is the subject matter of the misconduct allegation would, if that conduct were proved, amount to—
>
> - (a) misconduct,
> - (b) gross misconduct, or
> - (c) neither.
>
> (3) The Commissioner must make the assessment under paragraph (2) as soon as reasonably practicable after the misconduct allegation is referred, received or otherwise comes to the Commissioner’s attention, having regard to the information available at that time.
>
> (4) Where the Commissioner assesses that the conduct would, if proved, amount to neither misconduct nor gross misconduct, the Commissioner must return the allegation to the Authority (with any such recommendations as are considered appropriate) and the Authority may—
>
> - (a) take no action,
> - (b) take improvement action, or
> - (c) refer the matter to the senior officer’s reporting officer to be dealt with in accordance with the Senior Officers’ Performance Regulations.
>
> (5) Where the Commissioner assesses that the conduct would, if proved, amount to misconduct, the Commissioner must decide whether the misconduct allegation is to be investigated.
>
> (6) Where the Commissioner assesses that the conduct would, if proved, amount to gross misconduct, the Commissioner must investigate the misconduct allegation.
>
> (7) If, following an assessment under paragraph (5), the Commissioner decides not to investigate the misconduct allegation, the Commissioner must send the allegation to the Authority (with such recommendations as are considered appropriate) and the Authority may—
>
> - (a) take no action,
> - (b) take improvement action, or
> - (c) refer the matter to the senior officer’s reporting officer to be dealt with in accordance with the Senior Officers’ Performance Regulations.
>
> (8) In making an assessment under paragraph (5) the Commissioner must take into account the public interest and whether the misconduct allegation—
>
> - (a) was made anonymously,
> - (b) was specific in time and location,
> - (c) can reasonably be inferred to be unfounded or frivolous in nature,
> - (d) appears, on the face of the allegation, to be either vexatious or malicious.
>
> (9) Where the Authority decides under this regulation to take no further action, take improvement action or to refer the matter to the senior officer’s reporting officer to be dealt with in accordance with the Senior Officers’ Performance Regulations, the Authority must notify the senior officer as soon as practicable.
>
> (10) The Commissioner may, at any time before sending a misconduct form to the senior officer in accordance with regulation 16(2), revise the assessment made under paragraph (2).
>
> **7C. Investigation by Commissioner**
>
> Where, following an assessment under regulation 7B, a misconduct allegation is to be investigated, the Commissioner must, as soon as reasonably practicable—
>
> - (a) appoint a member of the Commissioner’s staff to carry out that investigation on the Commissioner’s behalf (the “investigator”),
> - (b) notify the Authority that the Commissioner is to carry out a misconduct investigation,
> - (c) keep the Authority informed, by giving further notices as the Commissioner considers appropriate, of any significant developments arising out of the misconduct investigation.
>
> **7D. Joint investigation by Commissioner and deputy chief constable**
>
> (1)  This regulation applies where—
>
> - (a) a misconduct investigation arises from facts and circumstances that concern both—
>   - (i) a senior officer to whom these Regulations apply, and
>   - (ii) a constable to whom the 2014 Conduct Regulations apply, and
> - (b) the Commissioner and the deputy chief constable each determine that a joint investigation is appropriate.
>
> (2) Nothing in this regulation—
>
> - (a) affects the separate statutory functions and duties of the Commissioner, and the investigator appointed, under these Regulations,
> - (b) affects the separate statutory functions and duties of the deputy chief constable, and the investigator appointed, under the 2014 Conduct Regulations,
> - (c) permits the substitution, modification or merging of any notice, procedure, report or decision required by these Regulations or the 2014 Conduct Regulations.
>
> (3) A joint investigation report must not be produced.
>
> (4) A joint investigation must not prejudice—
>
> - (a) the procedural fairness to the constables involved in the investigation, in particular—
>   - (i) the right to make representations,
>   - (ii) the right to be represented,
> - (b) the ability of independent determinations to be reached under these Regulations and the 2014 Conduct Regulations.
>
> **7E. Conducting joint investigations**
>
> (1) Where the Commissioner and the deputy chief constable determine that a joint investigation is appropriate, the Commissioner and the deputy chief constable must agree and record in writing a joint investigation strategy to determine—
>
> - (a) the scope of any shared lines of enquiry,
> - (b) whether, in relation to each witness, any interview would more appropriately be conducted—
>   - (i) jointly, by the investigator appointed under these Regulations and the investigator appointed under the 2014 Conduct Regulations, or
>   - (ii) separately, with records or summaries shared between investigators,
> - (c) any safeguarding or welfare considerations in relation to any witness or victim involved in the investigation, including ensuring that any witness or victim is treated in a way that accords with trauma-informed practice.
>
> (2) A joint investigation strategy must state—
>
> - (a) whether a witness will be interviewed jointly or separately by each investigator,
> - (b) the reasons for that decision,
> - (c) the arrangements for sharing documents, audio recordings, written records, evidence or other materials.
>
> **7F. Joint interviews**
>
> (1) A joint interview of a constable and a senior officer must not take place.
>
> (2) A joint interview of a witness may be conducted where—
>
> - (a) the Commissioner and the deputy chief constable agree,
> - (b) the approach is consistent with trauma-informed practice,
> - (c) the rights of each constable under the applicable Regulations are not prejudiced.
>
> (3) A joint interview of a witness must—
>
> - (a) be audio recorded unless both investigators agree that doing so would be inappropriate, in which case a single agreed written record must be prepared,
> - (b) be made available in the appropriate form to both investigators for the purposes of preparation of their respective investigator’s reports.
>
> **7G. Joint investigations: information sharing**
>
> Investigators may share—
>
> - (a) records of interviews (audio or written),
> - (b) witness statements,
> - (c) relevant documents,
> - (d) an investigator’s report, and
> - (e) any other material required for the conduct of each investigation,
>
> including information relating to the matters specified in paragraphs (a) to (e).

.

### 30. Accelerated Misconduct Hearings

After Part 3 (misconduct hearings), insert—

> **PART 3A — Accelerated Misconduct Hearings**
>
> **24A. General**
>
> Any time period specified in this Part in relation to an accelerated misconduct hearing may be reduced by agreement between the Commissioner and the chairing member of the panel appointed in accordance with regulation 24H(2)(a).
>
> **24B. Referral of case to accelerated misconduct hearing**
>
> (1) Subject to paragraph (2), after receipt of the investigator’s report under regulation 14(1), the Commissioner may at any time determine whether the special conditions specified in paragraph (4) are satisfied.
>
> (2) Where the case is referred to a misconduct hearing, the Commissioner must not make a determination under paragraph (1) on or after the date of the misconduct hearing.
>
> (3) On receipt of a statement submitted by the investigator under regulation 14(4), the Commissioner must determine whether the special conditions are satisfied.
>
> (4) The special conditions are that—
>
> - (a) there is sufficient evidence, in the form of written statements or other documents, to establish on the balance of probabilities that the conduct of the senior officer constitutes gross misconduct, and
> - (b) it is in the public interest for the senior officer to cease to hold office without delay.
>
> (5) In a case where a misconduct hearing or an accelerated misconduct hearing has been suspended or postponed by virtue of regulation 7(1), as soon as practicable after—
>
> - (a) the appropriate prosecutor intimates to the Commissioner that criminal proceedings are not to be brought in respect of any matter mentioned in the misconduct allegation, or
> - (b) any criminal proceedings which have been brought have been concluded,
>
> the Commissioner may make a determination, or in the case of an accelerated misconduct hearing must make a further determination, as to whether the special conditions are satisfied.
>
> (6) Where the Commissioner determines that the special conditions are satisfied, unless the Commissioner considers that the circumstances are such as to make it inappropriate to do so, the Commissioner must certify the case as one where the special conditions are satisfied and refer it to an accelerated misconduct hearing.
>
> (7) Where the Commissioner determines—
>
> - (a) that the special conditions are not satisfied, or
> - (b) that, although those conditions are satisfied, the circumstances are such as to make such certification inappropriate,
>
> the Commissioner must, if the investigation was incomplete, return the case to the investigator to complete the investigation or, in any other case, proceed in accordance with regulation 15 and Part 3.
>
> (8) Where the Commissioner is to proceed in accordance with regulation 15 and Part 3, regulation 15(1) must be read as if the words “Subject to regulation 24B, on receipt of the investigator’s report,” were omitted.
>
> (9) In this regulation, “**appropriate prosecutor**” has the same meaning as in regulation 7.
>
> **24C. Remission of case**
>
> (1) At any time after the case has been referred to an accelerated misconduct hearing but before the beginning of that hearing, the Commissioner may direct that the case be dealt with in accordance with regulation 15 and Part 3 if the Commissioner considers that the special conditions are no longer satisfied.
>
> (2) Where a direction is made under paragraph (1), the senior officer must be notified before the end of 4 working days beginning with the date on which that direction is made and the Commissioner must proceed in accordance with regulation 15 and Part 3.
>
> (3) Where the Commissioner is to proceed in accordance with regulation 15 and Part 3, regulation 15(1) must be read as if the words “Subject to regulation 24B, on receipt of the investigator’s report,” were omitted.
>
> **24D. Notice of referral to accelerated misconduct hearing**
>
> (1) Where a case is certified under regulation 24B(6) as one where the special conditions are satisfied and referred to an accelerated misconduct hearing, the Commissioner must, unless paragraph (2) applies, as soon as practicable give the senior officer notice of these matters and must supply the senior officer with a copy of—
>
> - (a) the certificate issued under regulation 24B(6),
> - (b) any statement or written representations the senior officer may have made to the investigator during the course of the investigation,
> - (c) the investigator’s report (together with any document attached to or referred to in that report as relates to the senior officer), and
> - (d) any other relevant document obtained during the course of the investigation.
>
> (2) This paragraph applies where the Commissioner considers that preventing the disclosure of any document mentioned in paragraph (1) is—
>
> - (a) necessary to avoid prejudicing current or future criminal proceedings,
> - (b) necessary for the purpose of the prevention or detection of crime or the apprehension or prosecution of offenders,
> - (c) necessary for the purpose of the prevention or detection of misconduct by other constables or police staff,
> - (d) necessary and proportionate for the protection of the welfare and safety of any informant or witness, or
> - (e) otherwise in the public interest.
>
> (3) Where any document is withheld by virtue of the application of paragraph (2), the Commissioner must, so far as is reasonably possible without prejudicing any of the matters mentioned in that paragraph, provide the senior officer with a summary of the content of that document.
>
> (4) The notice given under paragraph (1) must—
>
> - (a) describe the conduct that is the subject matter of the case and how that conduct is alleged to amount to gross misconduct,
> - (b) specify that the Commissioner will address the proceedings under regulation 24L(5),
> - (c) notify the senior officer of the effect of regulations 4 and 5,
> - (d) notify the senior officer of the right to seek advice from a staff association.
>
> (5) For the purposes of paragraph (1), a notice is to be treated as having been given to the senior officer—
>
> - (a) if given to the senior officer in person, on the date on which it is given,
> - (b) if sent to the senior officer by email or by any other means of electronic communication, on the date on which it is sent,
> - (c) if left with a person at the senior officer’s last known address, on the date on which it is left,
> - (d) if sent to the senior officer’s last known address by first class post using a recorded delivery service or any other postal service which provides for recorded delivery the next working day, on the second working day after the date on which it is sent.
>
> (6) Where a notice is required to be given to the senior officer under paragraph (1), it may instead be given to—
>
> - (a) a police representative chosen by the senior officer in accordance with regulation 4, or
> - (b) a legal representative acting for the senior officer in accordance with regulation 5,
>
> and any such giving is to be treated for the purposes of this regulation (including paragraph (5)) as giving the notice to the senior officer.
>
> **24E. Notice of accelerated misconduct hearing**
>

>
> - (a) notify the senior officer of the date, time and place of that hearing, and
> - (b) notify the senior officer of the effect of regulations 4 and 5 in relation to an accelerated misconduct hearing,
> - (c) notify the senior officer of the names of the persons appointed to the panel in accordance with regulation 24H(1).

> **24F. Public notification of accelerated misconduct hearing**
>
> (1) The chairing member of the panel may require the Authority to give notice of the hearing which contains information relating to one or more of—
>
> - (a) the name of the senior officer,
> - (b) the date of the hearing,
> - (c) the time of the hearing,
> - (d) the place at which the hearing will take place, and
> - (e) the conduct that is the subject matter of the case and how that conduct is alleged to amount to gross misconduct, as set out in the notice given in accordance with regulation 24D(1).
>
> (2) Where the chairing member of the panel requires notice to be given in accordance with paragraph (1), the Authority must publish the notice on its website as soon as practicable after notice of the hearing is given under regulation 24E(1).
>
> (3) Any person to whom this paragraph applies may make written representations to the chairing member of the panel in relation to—
>
> - (a) whether, and if so the extent to which, the chairing member of the panel should exclude any person from the whole or part of the hearing under regulation 24K(2)(a),
> - (b) whether the chairing member of the panel should impose any conditions under regulation 24K(2)(b),
> - (c) whether the chairing member of the panel should give directions prohibiting the publication of any matter relating to the proceedings under regulation 24K(2)(c),
> - (d) in light of the representations made under sub-paragraphs (a) to (c)—
>   - (i) whether the chairing member of the panel should require notice to be given under paragraph (1),
>   - (ii) which types of information mentioned in paragraph (1)(a) to (e) should be included in any such notice.
>
> (4) Paragraph (3) applies to—
>
> - (a) the senior officer,
> - (b) where the proceedings have arisen out of a complaint made by a member of the public, a constable, a member of the Authority’s staff or a member of police staff, the person who made the complaint, and
> - (c) the Commissioner.
>
> (5) Any written representations made in accordance with paragraph (3) must be provided no later than the date specified by the chairing member of the panel for provision of such representations.
>
> (6) The chairing member of the panel must notify each person listed in paragraph (4) of the date by which any written representations under paragraph (3) must be provided.
>
> **24G. Procedure on receipt of notice**
>
> (1) Before the end of 8 working days beginning with the date on which the senior officer is notified in accordance with regulation 24D(1), the senior officer must give the Commissioner and the chairing member of the panel—
>
> - (a) written notice of whether or not they accept that their conduct amounts to gross misconduct,
> - (b) where they accept that their conduct amounts to gross misconduct, any written submission they wish to make in mitigation,
> - (c) where they do not accept that their conduct amounts to gross misconduct, written notice of—
>   - (i) the allegations they dispute and their account of the relevant events, and
>   - (ii) any arguments on points of law they wish to be considered by the chairing member of the panel,
> - (d) a copy of any document they intend to rely on at the accelerated misconduct hearing.
>
> (2) If the senior officer does not provide the written notice required under paragraph (1)(a) to (d), the Commissioner may refer the misconduct allegation to an accelerated misconduct hearing notwithstanding the senior officer’s failure to provide the written notice.
>
> **24H. Appointment of panel**
>
> (1)  Where a case is—
>
> - (a) certified under regulation 24B(6) as one where the special conditions are satisfied, and
> - (b) referred to an accelerated misconduct hearing,
>
> a panel of 3 individuals must be appointed to conduct the accelerated misconduct hearing.
>
> (2) A panel under paragraph (1) must comprise—
>
> - (a) a chairing member, appointed by the Lord President from the legal members of the First-tier Tribunal, and
> - (b) two members appointed by the Authority—
>   - (i) one member who is a senior expert in policing, and
>   - (ii) one independent lay member, who the Authority considers has the appropriate skills, knowledge and expertise to serve on the panel.
>
> (3) The senior expert in policing must not previously have worked directly with the senior officer.
>
> (4) The chairing member of the panel must take appropriate action to ensure the efficient and effective bringing of proceedings and that they are conducted in a timely, fair and transparent manner.
>
> (5) An individual appointed to serve on the panel must declare to the person appointing them any conflicts of interest which may arise from appointment to a panel.
>
> (6) The Lord President, or the Authority, as appropriate, may not appoint to a panel—
>
> - (a) a member of the Authority,
> - (b) a member of the Authority’s staff,
> - (c) a member of any committee of the Authority established under paragraph 9(1) of schedule 1 of the Act,
> - (d) any individual whose appointment could give rise to a reasonable concern as to whether that individual could act impartially in relation to the misconduct hearing.
>
> (7) The chairing member of the panel may appoint up to two individuals to act as assessors to assist the panel.
>
> (8) An assessor appointed under paragraph (7) may provide the panel with such advice and assistance as the panel requests, but may not otherwise participate in the proceedings.
>
> (9) Where a panel has been constituted under this regulation, a determination of the panel—
>
> - (a) is valid if made by a majority,
> - (b) must not disclose whether it was unanimous or by a majority.
>
> (10) The Authority must pay panel members such remuneration and allowances as the Authority, with the approval of the Scottish Ministers, determines.
>
> (11) In this regulation, “**legal member**” means an individual holding membership of the First-tier Tribunal in accordance with section 15(2) of the Tribunals (Scotland) Act 2014[^f00008].
>
> **24I. Documents to be supplied**
>
> As soon as reasonably practicable prior to the accelerated misconduct hearing, the Commissioner must supply the chairing member of the panel with a copy of—
>
> - (a) the notice given to the senior officer under regulation 24D(1),
> - (b) the other documents given to the senior officer under regulation 24D(1), and
> - (c) the documents provided by the senior officer under regulation 24G(1).
>
> **24J. Proceedings in senior officer’s absence**
>
> (1) Where the senior officer informs the chairing member of the panel that the senior officer is unable to attend the accelerated misconduct hearing on grounds which the chairing member considers to be reasonable, including the senior officer’s welfare, the chairing member may permit the senior officer to participate in the proceedings by live television link or other suitable remote means.
>
> (2) Paragraph (3) applies where the senior officer—
>
> - (a) participates in the accelerated misconduct hearing in accordance with paragraph (1), or
> - (b) otherwise does not attend that hearing.
>
> (3) Where this paragraph applies—
>
> - (a) the senior officer may be represented at the accelerated misconduct hearing by the senior officer’s police representative, an advocate or a solicitor, and
> - (b) the hearing may proceed and conclude in the senior officer’s absence, whether or not the senior officer is so represented.
>
> **24K. Reporting restrictions, participation and exclusions from accelerated misconduct hearing**
>
> (1) Subject to paragraph (2), an accelerated misconduct hearing must be held in public.
>
> (2) Having considered any representations received under regulation 24F(3), the chairing member of the panel may—
>
> - (a) in relation to the attendance at the hearing of a person under this regulation, exclude any person as they see fit from the whole or a part of it,
> - (b) impose such conditions as they see fit relating to the attendance of any person at the hearing in order to facilitate the proper conduct of it, and
> - (c) give such directions as they think appropriate prohibiting the publication of any matter relating to the hearing.
>
> (3) Where it appears to the chairing member of the panel that any person may, in giving evidence, disclose information, being information to which paragraph (4) applies, which ought not to be disclosed to any person attending the proceedings (other than a party to the proceedings) the chairing member must require such attending persons to withdraw while the evidence is given.
>
> (4) This paragraph applies to information insofar as the chairing member of the panel considers that preventing disclosure of it to an attendee is—
>
> - (a) necessary for the purpose of preventing the premature or inappropriate disclosure of information that is relevant to, or may be used in, any criminal proceedings,
> - (b) necessary for the purpose of the prevention or detection of crime, or the apprehension or prosecution of offenders,
> - (c) necessary for the purpose of the prevention or detection of misconduct by other constables or police staff,
> - (d) necessary and proportionate for the protection of the welfare and safety of any informant or witness, or
> - (e) otherwise in the public interest.
>
> (5) Where the accelerated misconduct hearing has arisen from a complaint made by a member of the public, a constable, a member of the Authority’s staff or a member of police staff, the chairing member of the panel must notify the person who made the complaint of the date, time and location of that hearing.
>
> **24L. Procedure at accelerated misconduct hearing**
>
> (1) Subject to the following paragraphs of this regulation, the chairing member of the panel is to determine the procedure at the accelerated misconduct hearing.
>
> (2) At the beginning of the accelerated misconduct hearing, the chairing member of the panel must give the senior officer the opportunity to say whether or not the senior officer accepts that the senior officer’s conduct amounts to gross misconduct.
>
> (3) Subject to paragraph (4), no witnesses other than the senior officer may give evidence at the accelerated misconduct hearing and the chairing member of the panel must determine whether and by whom the senior officer can be questioned.
>
> (4) The investigator, or suitable replacement with sufficient knowledge of the investigation to be able to answer questions, must attend the accelerated misconduct hearing at the request of the chairing member of the panel to answer questions.
>
> (5) The Commissioner must address the proceedings for the purpose of—
>
> - (a) presenting the case against the senior officer,
> - (b) responding to any view expressed at the proceedings, and
> - (c) addressing the panel on any matter arising under these Regulations.
>
> (6) Where the Commissioner is required to address the proceedings under paragraph (5), the Commissioner may be represented for that purpose by a solicitor or advocate.
>
> (7) The senior officer, or their representative, may address the proceedings in order to—
>
> - (a) present the case of the senior officer,
> - (b) respond to any view expressed at the proceedings, and
> - (c) address the panel on any matter arising under these Regulations.
>
> (8) An audio recording may be made of the accelerated misconduct hearing and, where such a recording is made, the senior officer must be provided with a copy of that recording as soon as reasonably practicable after the conclusion of the hearing.
>
> (9) Where no audio recording is made under paragraph (8), a written record of the accelerated misconduct hearing must be prepared and—
>
> - (a) the chairing member of the panel must provide a draft of that record to the senior officer,
> - (b) the senior officer must be given the opportunity to make representations in relation to that draft,
> - (c) the chairing member of the panel must consider any representations made, and
> - (d) having considered those representations, if any, the chairing member of the panel must send the final written record of the accelerated misconduct hearing to the senior officer.
>
> **24M. Determination and disciplinary action**
>
> (1) At the conclusion of the accelerated misconduct hearing, the panel must—
>
> - (a) determine whether the conduct which is the subject matter of the misconduct allegation is the conduct of the senior officer,
> - (b) determine whether it is established that any conduct of the senior officer amounts to gross misconduct.
>
> (2) A determination under paragraph (1) is to be made on the balance of probabilities.
>
> (3) Where the panel has determined that the conduct of the senior officer does not amount to gross misconduct, the panel may—
>
> - (a) dismiss the case, or
> - (b) return the case to be dealt with in accordance with regulation 15 and Part 3.
>
> (4) Where the panel has determined that the conduct of the senior officer amounts to gross misconduct, the panel must impose disciplinary action which, subject to the provisions of this regulation, may be—
>
> - (a) a final written warning,
> - (b) dismissal with such notice as the panel may specify, or
> - (c) dismissal without notice.
>
> (5) Where, on the date of the determination under paragraph (1)(b), the senior officer was subject to a final written warning which was in effect, a final written warning may not be given.
>
> (6) A final written warning remains in effect for a period of 2 years from the date on which it is given.
>
> (7) Where a final written warning is given under paragraph (4)(a), the period in paragraph (6) may be extended by a panel conducting any subsequent accelerated misconduct hearing to a maximum period of 5 years.
>
> **24N. Notification of outcome and publication**
>
> (1) The chairing member of the panel must, as soon as reasonably practicable and not later than 10 working days from the date of conclusion of the accelerated misconduct hearing, submit a report to the Authority setting out—
>
> - (a) the determination of the panel under regulation 24M(1),
> - (b) the reasons for the determination,
> - (c) any disciplinary action imposed under regulation 24M(4), and
> - (d) where it was determined that the conduct of the senior officer amounted to gross misconduct but the disciplinary action imposed was not dismissal without notice, the reasons for that decision.
>
> (2) Before the report is submitted to the Authority, the chairing member of the panel must, having regard to paragraph (4), consider, separately in relation to—
>
> - (a) submission to the Authority,
> - (b) disclosure to the senior officer,
> - (c) disclosure to any person under paragraph (7), and
> - (d) publication under paragraph (9),
>
> whether any information contained in the report should be withheld, redacted or anonymised.
>
> (3) Where the chairing member of the panel determines that information should be withheld, redacted or anonymised for any purpose specified in paragraph (2), the chairing member of the panel must—
>
> - (a) record the reasons for that determination, including the category of disclosure to which it applies, and
> - (b) ensure that the appropriate version of the report is prepared and used for that purpose.
>
> (4) In considering the matters mentioned in paragraph (2), the chairing member of the panel may produce different versions of the report where the chairing member of the panel considers this to be necessary and proportionate, having regard to—
>
> - (a) the nature of the information concerned,
> - (b) the purpose of the disclosure,
> - (c) the rights and interests of any data subject.
>
> (5) A report submitted to the Authority under paragraph (1) must—
>
> - (a) in a case where a final written warning has been given, explain the effect of regulation 24M(5), (6) and (7) in relation to any future misconduct allegation,
> - (b) include notice of the senior officer’s right to appeal the decision of the panel to impose disciplinary action to the First-tier Tribunal under section 56(1) or (1A) of the Act and the procedure for making such an appeal.
>
> (6) As soon as practicable after receiving the report, the Authority must notify the senior officer of the outcome of the accelerated misconduct hearing by providing a copy of the report, subject to such withholding, redaction or anonymisation as the chairing member of the panel considers necessary in accordance with paragraph (2)(b).
>
> (7) The Authority must provide a copy of the report to—
>
> - (a) the Commissioner,
> - (b) where the accelerated misconduct hearing arose from a complaint made by a member of the public, a constable, a member of the Authority’s staff or a member of police staff, the person who made the complaint,
>
> subject to such withholding, redaction or anonymisation as the chairing member of the panel considers necessary in accordance with paragraph (2)(c).
>
> (8) In determining what information may be withheld, redacted or anonymised for the purposes of paragraph (7), the chairing member of the panel must have particular regard to whether the information—
>
> - (a) comprises personal data relating to the senior officer or any other individual which is not necessary to enable the recipient to understand the outcome and reasoning of the panel, or
> - (b) relates to private or personal circumstances which do not materially affect the determination or disciplinary outcome.
>
> (9) Subject to paragraph (11), the chairing member of the panel must require the Authority to publish a version of the report.
>
> (10) In relation to publication, the chairing member of the panel may only withhold, redact or anonymise information in the report—
>
> - (a) insofar as the chairing member of the panel considers this is—
>   - (i) necessary for the purpose of preventing the premature or inappropriate disclosure of information that is relevant to, or may be used in, criminal proceedings,
>   - (ii) necessary for the purpose of the prevention or detection of crime, or the apprehension or prosecution of offenders,
>   - (iii) necessary for the purpose of the prevention or detection of misconduct by other constables or police staff,
>   - (iv) necessary and proportionate for the protection of the welfare and safety of any informant or witness, or
>   - (v) otherwise in the public interest, and
> - (b) in line with restrictions imposed on the disclosure of information during the course of proceedings.
>
> (11) The chairing member of the panel may dispense with the duty to require publishing of the report under paragraph (9) where, having regard to the matters set out in paragraph (10), the chairing member of the panel considers publication to be inappropriate in the particular circumstances of the case.
>
> (12) In making a decision under paragraph (11), the chairing member of the panel may have regard to any representations made during the accelerated misconduct hearing.
>
> (13) Information that has already lawfully been published during the course of the proceedings may not be withheld, redacted or anonymised for the purposes of publication under this regulation.
>
> (14) Where the Authority is required to publish the report under paragraph (9), it must do so—
>
> - (a) as soon as practicable after the senior officer is notified under paragraph (6),
> - (b) on its website for a period of 28 days.

.

EXPLANATORY NOTE

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the Police Service of Scotland (Senior Officers) (Conduct) Regulations 2013 (“**the 2013 Regulations**”), which make provision for the procedures to be followed where the conduct of a senior officer of the Police Service of Scotland is alleged to amount to misconduct or gross misconduct.

Part 1 makes provision on the citation, commencement and interpretation of these Regulations. Part 2 (regulations 3 to 32) amends the 2013 Regulations.

Regulation 4 amends the interpretation provisions in regulation 2 of the 2013 Regulations by omitting, modifying, substituting and inserting a number of defined terms, including new definitions for accelerated misconduct hearings, misconduct pre-hearings, special conditions and trauma-informed practice, and by replacing the definition of “senior officer” to address cases where a constable is subsequently promoted to senior officer after the conduct occurred.

Regulation 5 amends regulation 3 so that the 2013 Regulations apply to conduct of a constable below the rank of assistant chief constable occurring on or after 26 November 2026 where the constable is subsequently promoted, on a permanent or temporary basis, to the rank of senior officer or was temporarily promoted at the time of the conduct.

Regulations 6 and 7 amend regulations 4 and 5 of the 2013 Regulations to prevent a police representative or legal representative from answering questions on behalf of a senior officer, and to extend the right to legal representation to misconduct pre‑hearings and accelerated misconduct hearings.

Regulation 8 amends the suspension provisions in regulation 6 of the 2013 Regulations, including providing a new power for the Police Investigations and Review Commissioner (“**the Commissioner**”) to recommend suspension to the Scottish Police Authority (“**the Authority**”) and updated suspension conditions requiring consideration of temporary redeployment as an alternative to suspension.

Regulation 9 amends regulation 7 of the 2013 Regulations to transfer responsibility for certain decisions relating to alleged criminal offences from the Authority to the Commissioner.

Regulation 10 inserts new regulations 7A to 7G into the 2013 Regulations. These create a new statutory framework within which all misconduct allegations must be referred to the Commissioner for assessment. They replace the previous preliminary assessment by the Authority with a new assessment by the Commissioner, and make provision for joint investigations where allegations concern both a senior officer and a constable subject to the Police Service of Scotland (Conduct) Regulations 2014. The new provisions require a written joint investigation strategy, regulate joint interviews, and provide for information-sharing while preserving the separate statutory roles and procedural safeguards under both sets of conduct regulations.

Regulations 11 to 14 omit regulations 8 to 11 of the 2013 Regulations, removing the previous preliminary assessment by the Authority and the Authority’s investigatory functions. Regulation 15 amends regulation 12 of the 2013 Regulations to require further notice of investigation where the investigator’s assessment of the seriousness of the conduct changes. Regulation 16 amends the investigator’s report provisions in regulation 14 of the 2013 Regulations to require the investigator to submit an early report where the investigator believes the special conditions for an accelerated misconduct hearing may be satisfied, or where new evidence suggests the conduct would not amount to misconduct or gross misconduct. In such cases the Commissioner must reassess the matter and either, where appropriate, bring the investigation to an end, or, where special conditions are satisfied, certify the case and refer it to an accelerated misconduct hearing in accordance with new regulation 24B of the 2013 Regulations.

Regulation 17 amends the provisions relating to referral to a misconduct hearing in regulation 15 of the 2013 Regulations to reflect the Commissioner’s new functions. Regulation 18 inserts new regulation 15A into the 2013 Regulations and requires the Commissioner to notify certain complainants where a misconduct allegation is determined to involve no case to answer, setting out the outcome and reasons, subject to limited exceptions permitting information to be withheld or restricted in the public interest.

Regulation 19 amends the arrangements for misconduct hearings in regulation 16 of the 2013 Regulations, including updated disclosure obligations, rules on when a misconduct form is treated as received, and provision enabling a hearing to proceed notwithstanding the senior officer’s failure to provide written notice in response to the misconduct form.

Regulation 20 amends regulation 17 of the 2013 Regulations to allow measures that support the effective participation of witnesses, including remote evidence, the use of screens, allowing for a supporter or giving evidence in private, where this can be done without prejudicing the fairness of proceedings. Regulation 21 inserts new regulation 17A into the 2013 Regulations allowing the public notification of misconduct hearings, and for representations to the panel chair.

Regulation 22 substitutes the existing provisions on appointment of panels in regulation 18 of the 2013 Regulations with updated arrangements whereby the Lord President appoints a legally qualified chairing member and the Authority appoints the other panel members. Regulation 23 inserts new regulations 18A and 18B into the 2013 Regulations, governing the role of the chairing member of the panel, including the power to hold misconduct pre-hearings and to manage procedural matters in advance of a hearing within specified time limits. Regulation 24 amends the procedure at misconduct hearings set out in regulation 19 of the 2013 Regulations, including requiring the Commissioner to present the case against the senior officer. Regulation 25 amends regulation 20 of the 2013 Regulations to permit the senior officer to attend remotely in certain circumstances. Regulation 26 replaces regulation 21 of the 2013 Regulations on attendance of third parties with new provision establishing public hearings, subject to powers to exclude persons, impose conditions, or prohibit publication where necessary. Regulation 27 makes minor amendments to regulation 22 of the 2013 Regulations on determinations.

Regulation 28 amends the disciplinary action provisions in regulation 23 of the 2013 Regulations to remove verbal warnings, to revise the periods for which written and final written warnings remain in effect, and to provide for the period of a final written warning to be extended up to a maximum of five years. Regulation 29 substitutes regulation 24 of the 2013 Regulations to revise the arrangements for notification and publication following misconduct hearings.

Regulation 30 inserts a new Part 3A (regulations 24A to 24N) into the 2013 Regulations, establishing accelerated misconduct hearings. These regulations make provision for certification of cases where special conditions are met, for the giving of notice, for the conduct of the hearing, for the disciplinary actions available, including dismissal without notice, and for publication of the report prepared in relation to the accelerated misconduct hearing.

Regulation 31 substitutes Part 4 (Appeals) of the 2013 Regulations to prescribe that appeals under section 56(1) or (1A) of the Police and Fire Reform (Scotland) Act 2012 lie to the First-tier Tribunal against panel decisions to impose disciplinary action following a misconduct or accelerated misconduct hearing, and specifies when such decisions are to be treated as made for the purposes of an appeal.

Regulation 32 substitutes regulation 28 of the 2013 Regulations, making provision for the payment by the Authority of reasonable expenses incurred by a senior officer in connection with misconduct and accelerated misconduct hearings. It provides that legal expenses covered by a private insurance policy accessible by the senior officer are not payable by the Authority. The regulation also provides for the taxation of expenses and permits the Authority to reimburse other reasonable expenses incurred in connection with proceedings under the 2013 Regulations.

Part 3, together with schedules 1 and 2, contains consequential amendments, including amendments to the First‑tier Tribunal for Scotland General Regulatory Chamber (Police Appeals) (Procedure) Regulations 2025 and the Police Service of Scotland (Vetting) Regulations 2026, and transitional and saving provisions ensuring that cases relating to conduct occurring before 26 November 2026 continue to be dealt with under the legislation in force immediately before that date.
