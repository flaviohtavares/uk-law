# Crofting and Scottish Land Court Act 2026, Schedule 1

> Text as at 2026-10-26.
>
> Extent: Scotland.

## Schedule 1 — The Scottish Land Court

*Extent: S*

(introduced by section 56(2))

### Part 1 — Administration and organisation

**Not yet in force.**

#### 1. Incorporation[^c-80d0e6dc2198d2926a9aa1692d474482]

(1) The Court is a body corporate with a common seal which is to be judicially noticed.

(2) Any order or other instrument purporting to be sealed by the common seal is to be received as evidence without further proof.

#### 2. Appointment of members and Chair of the Court[^c-4a5756b33368cb940f36af18f54a65c0]

(1) The Court is to consist of such persons as His Majesty, on the recommendation of the First Minister, may appoint as members.

(2) One of the persons appointed by His Majesty is to be appointed as the Chair of the Court.

(3) Before recommending the appointment of a person as the Chair, the First Minister must consult the Lord President of the Court of Session.

(4) A person appointed as the Chair has the same rank and tenure of office as a judge of the Court of Session.

(5) The Chair is to be a person who, at the date of appointment—

- (a) is a sheriff principal or sheriff who has held office as such for a continuous period of not less than 10 years,
- (b) is a member of the Faculty of Advocates of not less than 10 years’ standing,
- (c) is a solicitor who, by virtue of [section 25A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/46/section/25A) (rights of audience) of the [Solicitors (Scotland) Act 1980](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/46/contents), has for a continuous period of not less than 10 years had a right of audience in the Court of Session, or
- (d) has, for a continuous period of not less than 10 years (discounting any period of relevant training) held a combination of the offices, membership and rights described in paragraphs (a) to (c).

(6) Sub-paragraph (5)(a) does not confer eligibility for appointment as Chair on a temporary sheriff principal or on a part-time sheriff who is not otherwise eligible for appointment.

(7) The membership of the court is to include a person who is able to speak the Gaelic language.

(8) The Scottish Ministers may by regulations make provision about the eligibility for appointment of members of the Court (other than the Chair).

(9) Regulations under sub-paragraph (8) are subject to the negative procedure.

#### 3. Designation and functions of Deputy Chair[^c-95ba93a43c2192115681e5b752d2015f]

(1) The Scottish Ministers may designate as a Deputy Chair any member who is eligible to hold the office of Chair.

(2) Sub-paragraphs (3) to (5) apply where no member is eligible to be designated as Deputy Chair.

(3) The Scottish Ministers may appoint as a Deputy Chair (and member of the Court) a person who is eligible under paragraph 2(5)(a) to hold the office of Chair.

(4) An appointment under sub-paragraph (3) is for such period as the Scottish Ministers may determine.

(5) Before making an appointment under sub-paragraph (3), the Scottish Ministers must consult the Lord President of the Court of Session.

(6) A Deputy Chair—

- (a) is to act in place of the Chair for such periods as the Chair may, with the consent of the Scottish Ministers, direct, and
- (b) may, when not acting in accordance with a direction under paragraph (a) or under sub-paragraph (7), perform the duties of a member of the Court other than the Chair.

(7) Where there is a vacancy in the office of Chair, or where the Chair is, for whatever reason, unable to act, the Deputy Chair is to act as Chair during such vacancy or inability to act if requested to do so by the Scottish Ministers.

(8) A Deputy Chair designated under sub-paragraph (1) or, as the case may be, appointed under sub-paragraph (3) is, while acting as Chair, to have the same powers and perform the same duties as the Chair.

#### 4. Principal clerk[^c-c34c2c3fed4500a8774daff4aac439ba]

The SCTS must appoint a suitable person to act as principal clerk to the Court.

#### 5. Remuneration[^c-80c5707a3f6ab574ee693d6d0c5759f1]

(1) The SCTS is to pay the Chair of the Court such salary and such allowances as the Treasury may determine.

(2) The SCTS is to pay each of the other members of the Court such salary and such allowances as the Scottish Ministers may determine.

(3) Sums required by the SCTS for the payment of a salary or an allowance under this paragraph are charged on the Scottish Consolidated Fund.

**6.**[^c-75bd916fbacc12ed838d782a47f5d69e] (1) The SCTS may pay to a member of the Court such sums as it may determine in respect of expenses reasonably incurred by the member in the performance of, or in connection with, the member’s duties.

(2) The SCTS may—

- (a) determine the circumstances in which such sums may be paid, and
- (b) determine different circumstances for different members.

**7.**[^c-fa41bf85ed7d27389d3e5140a4cba296] (1) The SCTS is to pay such remuneration as appears to the Scottish Ministers to be reasonable in all the circumstances to—

- (a) the Deputy Chair in respect of the additional functions of being Deputy Chair,
- (b) persons nominated under paragraph 13.

(2) The SCTS may pay to the Deputy Chair such allowances as it may determine.

(3) The SCTS may pay to each of the following persons such sums as it may determine in respect of expenses reasonably incurred by the person in the performance of, or in connection with, the person’s duties—

- (a) the Deputy Chair,
- (b) persons nominated under paragraph 13.

(4) The SCTS may—

- (a) determine the circumstances in which such sums may be paid, and
- (b) determine different circumstances for different persons.

**8.**[^c-7f094527191dd3965a0816c5cfc73b0a] Expenditure incurred by the Court in the performance of its functions may be paid by the SCTS.

#### 9. Quorum and delegation[^c-097cf0b31564cf58703bf5de49f0d9a8]

Subject to paragraph 10, a quorum of the Court is to be—

- (a) the Chair and one other member, or
- (b) three of its members, if none of them is the Chair.

**10.**[^c-134dc06960b7c39c1b4eef42f004b2e6] (1) The Court may delegate such of its powers as it may think expedient to any member or to any 2 members, with or without the assistance of one or more land valuers, assessors or other skilled persons, and may revoke, alter or modify any such delegation of powers.

(2) Subject to any regulations made under paragraph 15(2)(a), any order or determination arrived at under a delegation made in pursuance of this paragraph, other than a delegation to or including a member described in sub-paragraph (3) (whether sitting alone or with others), is on appeal subject to review by such a member sitting with 2 or more members, or nominated former members, of the Court sitting together.

(3) The members are—

- (a) the Chair,
- (b) a Deputy Chair,
- (c) a member designated by the Chair for the purposes of this paragraph as being suitably qualified by virtue of the member—
  - (i) having held judicial office prior to becoming a member, or
  - (ii) otherwise having sufficient relevant legal experience to hear and determine cases alone, or
- (d) a member of the Lands Tribunal for Scotland appointed in accordance with section 2(2A)(a) of the 1949 Act who is hearing and determining proceedings and other matters by virtue of section 57(2) of this Act.

**11.**[^c-cfbdad401fef26b65251874efca06aa4] On any question being determined by the Court, whether or not by virtue of paragraph 10, the Chair is to have a casting vote.

**12.**[^c-97ca6e49feec269e795ef2660b481b33] The reference in paragraph 10(2) to nominated former members is to such members as have vacated office, whether under [section 26](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/8/section/26) of the [Judicial Pensions and Retirement Act 1993](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/8/contents) or otherwise, and in relation to the particular review have been nominated under this paragraph by the principal clerk.

#### 13. Eligibility to act as members of the Upper Tribunal for Scotland[^c-f87fa840968f8aca35a5c138db0247f6]

The Chair may determine that a member of the Court is suitably qualified to be eligible to act as a member of the Upper Tribunal for Scotland.

#### 14. Fitness for office and removal of members of the Court[^c-8825f525c4c0668b7d6ac9d9ec6a58eb]

(1) Sections 21 to 25 of the Courts Reform (Scotland) Act 2014 (which provide for the convening of a Tribunal to consider fitness for office, suspension and removal of judicial office holders) apply to a member of the Court, other than the Chair, as they apply to the holder of a judicial office listed in section 21(3)(a) to (c) of that Act.

(2) For the purpose of the application of section 21(4)(b) of the Courts Reform (Scotland) Act 2014, “the relevant judicial office” for an investigation into whether an individual is fit to hold the office of member of the Court, is that office.

### Part 2 — Jurisdiction of the Court

**Not yet in force.**

#### 15. General[^c-03b429fef4916da7ea0e46c3d70705b2]

(1) The competence and jurisdiction of the Court comprises—

- (a) the matters and appeals described or referred to in this Part, and
- (b) any other matter or appeal conferred upon it by an enactment (whenever passed or made).

(2) The Scottish Ministers may by regulations—

- (a) specify or describe matters in respect of which paragraph 10(2) is not to apply,
- (b) modify this Part of the schedule to adjust what is (or is not) within the competence and jurisdiction of the Court,
- (c) modify paragraph 31 in respect of the method by which any proceedings before the Court relating to such matters as are specified in the regulations are to be stated or appealed to the Court of Session.

(3) Before making regulations under this paragraph, the Scottish Ministers must consult—

- (a) the Lord President of the Court of Session, and
- (b) the Chair.

(4) Regulations under this paragraph—

- (a) may (without limit to section 64 or 65) modify any enactment in consequence of a change made to the competence and jurisdiction of the Court or right of appeal (by whatever means) from the Court,
- (b) are subject to the affirmative procedure.

#### 16. Access rights[^c-13da45040cb70fa3e46f81f7af16fcae]

All matters, whether of fact or law, arising under the following sections of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003—

- (a) section 14 (prohibition signs, obstructions, dangerous impediments etc.),
- (b) section 28 (judicial determination of existence and extent of access rights and rights of way).

#### 17. Agriculture and agricultural holdings[^c-31d43390cd0bf9e738f859d70bf1be1f]

The jurisdiction set out in section 60 of the Agricultural Holdings (Scotland) Act 1991.

#### 18. Arbitration and compensation[^c-8c8a479b6e2225ff57b830142f173b76]

(1) Any question which is by any Act (including a local or private Act) directed, in whatever terms, to be determined by a person or one or more persons selected from either of the following panels—

- (a) the panel of official arbitrators appointed under the Acquisition of Land (Assessment of Compensation) Act 1919, and
- (b) the panel of referees appointed under Part I of the Finance (1909-10) Act 1910,

or which is so directed to be determined in the absence of agreement to the contrary.

(2) Any other question of disputed compensation under the Lands Clauses Acts, where the claim is for the injurious affection of any land.

(3) Any question arising as to the apportionment mentioned in section 116 of the Lands Clauses Consolidation Act 1845 or section 109 of the Lands Clauses Consolidation (Scotland) Act 1845, of any rent charge or other matter to which that section applies.

(4) Any question on which, but for this provision, an appeal or reference to the county court would or might be made by virtue of section 62 or 87 of the Local Government Act 1948.

**19.**[^c-033126e8e2fd47ab8bcd8db3115e812b] The Court may exercise any other jurisdiction conferred by any Act (including a local or private Act), or instrument made under any such Act, on a person or one or more persons selected as mentioned in paragraph 18(1)(a).

**20.**[^c-303b47806106080afd4f97dc697ea7c9] The Court may act as arbitrator under a reference by consent, and any agreement entered into before the commencement of the 1949 Act which provides for referring any matter to arbitration by a person or one or more persons is to, subject to any subsequent agreement, have effect as if it provided for referring the matter to arbitration by the Court.

#### 21. Community rights to buy: appeals[^c-28691a9105d3e88cdb16d014fb41e345]

Appeals made under the following sections of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003—

- (a) section 60A(4) (liability of owner of land for valuation expenses),
- (b) section 61 (community right to buy: appeals),
- (c) section 91 (crofting right to buy: appeals),
- (d) section 97V (community right to buy abandoned, neglected or detrimental land: appeals).

**22.**[^c-066d4327fd46e8c8e8d1a8293a9f4d96] Appeals made under section 69 (right to buy land for further sustainable development: appeals) of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2016.

#### 23. Crofting and crofting community right to buy[^c-3ef057eb1acd16a1f81632f077c1a2dc]

(1) All matters, whether of fact or law, which arise under the [Crofters (Scotland) Act 1993](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1993/44/contents).

(2) Except to the extent otherwise provided in Part 3 of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 (crofting community right to buy), all matters, whether of fact or law, and appeals, which arise under that Part.

#### 24. March Dykes, runrig and commonties[^c-4a0596568863ef1a493524912079203e]

All matters, whether of fact or law, which arise under—

- (a) the March Dykes Act 1661,
- (b) the March Dykes Act 1669,
- (c) Runrig Lands Act 1695,
- (d) Division of Commonties Act 1695,

and that notwithstanding any provision in those Acts to the contrary.

#### 25. Small landholdings[^c-60c21d9c80b599bcd146af216136765d]

All matters, whether of fact or law, which arise under the Small Landholders (Scotland) Acts 1886 to 1931 or schedule 2 of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2025 (small landholdings).

#### 26. Succession to certain leases[^c-b81e8f1f578cebbcd5cc7a53a3708ba2]

Applications under section 16 (provisions relating to leases) of the Succession (Scotland) Act 1964.

### Part 3 — Rules, procedure, expenses and powers

#### 27. Rules of court[^c-a6cdba4c23b3a209092382664d807e8a][^c-79c60e975a10893210be83bc7e3961dd]

(1) The Court of Session may by act of sederunt make provision for or about—

- (a) the procedure and practice to be followed in proceedings in the Court,
- (b) any matter incidental or ancillary to such proceedings.

(2) An act of sederunt under sub-paragraph (1) may make—

- (a) incidental, supplemental, consequential, transitional, transitory or saving provision,
- (b) provision amending, repealing or revoking any enactment (including any provision of this Act) relating to matters with respect to which an act of sederunt under sub-paragraph (1) may be made,
- (c) different provision for different purposes.

(3) Before making an act of sederunt under sub-paragraph (1) with respect to any matter, the Court of Session must—

- (a) consult the Chair,
- (b) consult the Scottish Civil Justice Council, and
- (c) take into consideration any views expressed by the Council with respect to that matter.

(4) Paragraphs (a) and (b) of sub-paragraph (3) do not apply in relation to an act of sederunt that embodies, with or without modifications, draft rules submitted by the Scottish Civil Justice Council to the Court of Session.

(5) This paragraph is without prejudice to—

- (a) any enactment that enables the Court of Session to make rules (by act of sederunt or otherwise) regulating the practice and procedure to be followed in proceedings to which this paragraph applies, or
- (b) the inherent powers of the Court.

#### 28. Procedure[^c-bfdd6336aa1b29c9a0768275d0dbb83f]

**Not yet in force.**

The Chair may—

- (a) select a member or members to deal with a particular case, type of case (whether by subject matter or otherwise) or group of cases, or
- (b) allocate members to a pool from which a member or members to deal with a particular case is to be selected (for example by reference to the subject matter of types of case), either by the Chair or a member designated by the Chair for that purpose.

**29.**[^c-acee22c1c90a429ee22d3f76660a0e89] Where a person is or may be liable for any compensation falling to be determined under section 57 or 97 of the Land Clauses Consolidation (Scotland) Act 1845 (which sections relate to the procedure in default of a claimant), the surveyor referred to in those sections is to be selected from members of the Court in accordance with the provisions of this schedule.

#### 30. Powers[^c-f4a656a59749edcc79b92c3430979583]

**Not yet in force.**

(1) The Court may—

- (a) ascertain the facts in any case—
  - (i) by hearing parties and examining witnesses,
  - (ii) by means of affidavits, or
  - (iii) by such other mode of inquiry as it deems appropriate, and
- (b) determine cases without an oral hearing.

(2) The Court may require the production of any document or information relating to a case and it may summon and examine on oath such witnesses as it thinks fit to call or allow to appear before it.

(3) The Court may, when sitting in open court, report in writing to the Lord Ordinary any person who has been guilty of contempt of court.

(4) Where a report has been made to a Lord Ordinary under sub-paragraph (3), the Lord Ordinary may punish such person as if the contempt had been committed in the Lord Ordinary’s court.

(5) The Chair, when sitting in open court, has the same power as a Lord Ordinary to punish for contempt of court.

(6) Despite sub-paragraph (1)(b), the Court may not determine without an oral hearing a disputed claim for compensation which—

- (a) is payable in respect of a compulsory acquisition of land, or
- (b) depends directly or indirectly on the value of any land,

without the consent of the person making the claim.

#### 31. Finality of decisions and special cases[^c-5d1e509c29618c71ae114be1a227726c]

**Not yet in force.**

(1) Subject to sub-paragraph (2), the decision of the Court in respect of a matter described in paragraphs 23 or 25 is final.

(2) Except as provided in sub-paragraph (3), the Court may, if it thinks fit, and must, on the request of a party, state a special case on any question of law arising in any proceedings before it under any enactment for the opinion of the Inner House of the Court of Session, which may finally determine that question.

(3) Sub-paragraph (2) does not apply in respect of proceedings in connection with any matter which may be determined by the Court by virtue of—

- (a) the [Agricultural Holdings (Scotland) Act 1991](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/55/contents),
- (b) the [Agricultural Holdings (Scotland) Act 2003](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2003/11/contents),
- (c) section 116 of the Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2016, or
- (d) the transfer of the jurisdiction and functions of the Lands Tribunal for Scotland under section 57(1).

#### 32. Expenses[^c-be6a27b95c8abddb5ee2fa71779134fb]

**Not yet in force.**

The Court may determine the amount of the expenses in any proceedings before it and the proportion to be borne by the different parties.

#### 33. Enforcement[^c-fe3600439d232906bb9fd7cf625e87e1]

**Not yet in force.**

An order or determination of the Court may be enforced as if it were a decree of the sheriff having jurisdiction in the area in which the order or determination is to be enforced.

#### 34. Accounts[^c-978730dfce82131f4a7ce9364992087e]

**Not yet in force.**

The Court is to submit such estimates and keep such accounts of its receipts and expenditure, and its accounts are to be audited in accordance with such regulations, as the Treasury may direct.

### Part 4 — Interpretation

#### 35. Interpretation[^c-281c86044813239668583b18753cb09f][^c-a008cd520b6f8fd9bfa8ebd02ecdd9ca]

In this schedule—

- “**Chair**” means the Chair of the Court appointed under paragraph 2(2),
- “**Court**” means the Scottish Land Court,
- “**the SCTS**” means the Scottish Courts and Tribunals Service,
- “**solicitor**” means a person enrolled as a solicitor in the roll of solicitors kept under section 7 of the Solicitors (Scotland) Act 1980,
- “**Upper Tribunal for Scotland**” means the Upper Tribunal for Scotland, established by section 1 of the Tribunals (Scotland) Act 2014.
